🔬 1. Atomic Mass and Composition

The nucleus consists of protons and neutrons (collectively called nucleons). Protons are positively charged, neutrons are neutral.

  • Atomic Number (Z): Number of protons.
  • Mass Number (A): Total number of nucleons (protons + neutrons). Number of neutrons N = A - Z.
  • Isotopes: Same Z, different A (e.g., Protium, Deuterium, Tritium).
  • Isobars: Same A, different Z.
  • Isotones: Same number of neutrons (N).
💡 Size and Density of Nucleus

Radius of nucleus: R = R0 A1/3 (where R0 ≈ 1.2 × 10-15 m).

Nuclear Density is independent of mass number A. It is constant for all nuclei (≈ 2.3 × 1017 kg/m3).

⚖️ 2. Mass Defect and Binding Energy

The rest mass of a nucleus is always slightly less than the sum of the rest masses of its constituent nucleons. This difference is called Mass Defect (Δm).

Δm = [Z mp + (A - Z) mn] - M

Binding Energy (BE): The energy equivalent to the mass defect. It is the energy required to break the nucleus into its constituent nucleons.

BE = Δm c2 (In MeV: BE = Δm × 931.5 MeV/u)

🚨 Exam Facts: Stability

The stability of a nucleus is determined by its Binding Energy per Nucleon (BE/A), not just total BE. Iron (Fe-56) has the maximum BE/A (≈ 8.8 MeV) and is the most stable nucleus.

☢️ 3. Radioactivity

The spontaneous emission of radiation (α, β, γ) from unstable nuclei to achieve stability.

Fig: Deflection of Radioactive emissions in an Electric Field

Law of Radioactive Decay

The rate of disintegration is directly proportional to the number of undecayed nuclei present at that instant.

dN/dt = -λ N ⇒ N = N0 e-λt

  • Half-Life (T1/2): Time in which half the nuclei decay. T1/2 = ln(2)/λ = 0.693/λ.
  • Mean Life (τ): Average life of all nuclei. τ = 1/λ = 1.44 T1/2.

💥 4. Nuclear Fission and Fusion

Nuclear Fission

The splitting of a heavy nucleus (like U-235) into two intermediate mass nuclei with the release of large amount of energy and neutrons. Basis of Nuclear Reactors and Atom Bomb.

Moderator: Slows down fast neutrons (e.g., Heavy water, Graphite).
Control Rods: Absorb excess neutrons to control chain reaction (e.g., Cadmium, Boron).

Nuclear Fusion

The combining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus with the release of a huge amount of energy. Basis of Hydrogen Bomb and energy generation in Stars (Sun).

Requires extremely high temperature (≈ 107 K) and pressure to overcome electrostatic repulsion between nuclei.
