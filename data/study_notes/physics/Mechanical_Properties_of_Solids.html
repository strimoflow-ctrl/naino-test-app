🛠️ 1. Elasticity and Plasticity

  • Elasticity: The property of a body by virtue of which it tends to regain its original size and shape when the applied force is removed. (e.g., steel, rubber).
  • Plasticity: The property by virtue of which it does not regain its original shape and size after removal of deforming force. (e.g., putty, mud).
🤯 Amazing Info: Steel vs Rubber

Did you know? Steel is more elastic than rubber! Elasticity is the resistance to deformation. Since a greater force is required to stretch steel than rubber for the same strain, steel possesses a higher Young's modulus and is technically more elastic.

📏 2. Stress and Strain

Stress (σ)

The restoring force per unit area set up inside the body. Unit: N/m2 or Pascal (Pa).

  • Normal Stress: Force applied perpendicular to surface. (Tensile: increases length; Compressive: decreases length).
  • Tangential/Shearing Stress: Force applied parallel to surface, causing a change in shape.
  • Volumetric/Hydraulic Stress: Force applied uniformly over entire surface, changing volume.

Strain (ε)

The ratio of change in dimension to the original dimension. It is a dimensionless quantity.

  • Longitudinal Strain: ΔL / L
  • Shear Strain: Δx / L = tanθ ≈ θ
  • Volumetric Strain: ΔV / V

🔗 3. Hooke's Law and Moduli of Elasticity

Hooke's Law: Within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain.

Stress ∝ Strain ⇒ Stress / Strain = E (Modulus of Elasticity).

💡 Types of Moduli
  • Young's Modulus (Y): Longitudinal Stress / Longitudinal Strain. Y = (F/A) / (ΔL/L) = FL / AΔL. Applicable to solids only.
  • Shear Modulus / Modulus of Rigidity (G): Shearing Stress / Shearing Strain. G = (F/A) / θ. Applicable to solids only.
  • Bulk Modulus (B): Hydraulic Stress / Volumetric Strain. B = -p / (ΔV/V). Applicable to solids, liquids, and gases.
📘 Compressibility (k)

It is the reciprocal of Bulk Modulus. k = 1 / B. Gases are highly compressible, hence their Bulk Modulus is very low.

📈 4. Stress-Strain Curve

Fig: Typical Stress-Strain Curve for a ductile metal
  • Proportional Limit: Region where Hooke's Law is strictly obeyed (straight line).
  • Elastic Limit (Yield Point): Maximum stress the material can endure without permanent set.
  • Plastic Region: Beyond elastic limit, strain increases much more rapidly than stress. Material undergoes permanent deformation.
  • Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS): Maximum stress the material can withstand before breaking.
  • Fracture Point: The point at which the wire breaks.
🚨 Exam Facts: Ductile vs Brittle

If the distance between UTS and Fracture Point is large, the material is Ductile (can be drawn into wires, e.g., Copper). If they are very close, the material is Brittle (breaks easily, e.g., Glass).

🔋 5. Elastic Potential Energy

When a wire is stretched, work is done against the restoring forces. This work is stored as elastic potential energy.

Work done (W) = ½ × Load × Extension = ½ F ΔL

💡 Energy Density

Energy stored per unit volume of the stretched wire is called Energy Density (u).

u = ½ × Stress × Strain = ½ × Y × (Strain)2

Poisson's Ratio (ν)

Ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain. ν = - (Δd/d) / (ΔL/L). Theoretical limit: -1 to 0.5. Practical limit: 0 to 0.5.
