Moving Charges and Magnetism

🧲 1. Magnetic Force on a Moving Charge

A charge q moving with velocity v in a magnetic field B experiences a magnetic Lorentz force.

F = q (v × B) ⇒ F = qvB sinθ

Force is perpendicular to both velocity and magnetic field. Hence, magnetic force does NO work (W=0) and cannot change the kinetic energy or speed of the particle. It only changes the direction.

Motion in a Uniform Magnetic Field

When θ = 90°, the particle moves in a circular path. The necessary centripetal force is provided by the magnetic force.

  • Radius (r): r = mv / qB = p / qB = √(2mK) / qB
  • Time Period (T): T = 2πm / qB (Independent of velocity and radius).
🤯 Amazing Info: Helical Path

If the velocity has a component parallel to B (i.e., θ is not 0, 90, or 180), the particle moves in a helical (spiral) path. The parallel component v cosθ moves it forward, and perpendicular component v sinθ moves it in a circle. The forward distance covered in one time period is called Pitch.

⚡ 2. Magnetic Force on a Current Carrying Conductor

A straight conductor of length L carrying current I placed in a uniform magnetic field B experiences a force:

F = I (L × B) ⇒ F = ILB sinθ

Force between two parallel conductors

Two parallel conductors carrying currents I1 and I2 separated by distance d experience a force per unit length:

💡 Force Formula

F/L = (μ0 / 2π) (I1I2 / d)

Rule: Like currents (same direction) attract each other. Unlike currents (opposite direction) repel each other.

📐 3. Biot-Savart Law

Gives the magnetic field dB produced by a small current element Idl at a point having position vector r.

dB = (μ0 / 4π) I (dl × r) / r3 ⇒ dB = (μ0 / 4π) I dl sinθ / r2

Applications:

  • Center of a circular coil: B = μ0NI / 2R.
  • Straight infinite wire: B = μ0I / 2πd.

🌀 4. Ampere's Circuital Law

The line integral of magnetic field B around any closed path in vacuum is equal to μ0 times the net steady current enclosed by the path.

∮ B • dl = μ0 Ienclosed

Applications:

  • Solenoid: A long tightly wound coil. Magnetic field inside is uniform and strong. B = μ0 n I (where n = number of turns per unit length = N/L). At the ends, B is half of the value at the center.
  • Toroid: A hollow circular ring with wire wound around it. Inside the core: B = μ0 n I. Outside and in the empty space: B = 0.

⏱️ 5. Moving Coil Galvanometer

A device used to detect small electric currents. Principle: A current carrying coil placed in a magnetic field experiences a torque.

Torque: τ = NIAB sinθ. Radial magnetic field is used to make θ = 90° always, so τ = NIAB.

Restoring torque of spring = kφ. At equilibrium: NIAB = kφ ⇒ φ ∝ I.

🚨 Exam Facts: Conversion of Galvanometer
  • To Ammeter: Connect a low resistance (Shunt) in parallel. Shunt Resistance S = Ig G / (I - Ig).
  • To Voltmeter: Connect a high resistance in series. Series Resistance R = (V / Ig) - G.
