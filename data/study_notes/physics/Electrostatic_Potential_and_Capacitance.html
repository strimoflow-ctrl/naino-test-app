Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Class 12 Physics | Chapter 2
🎯 Target: NEET UG | Fully NCERT Based

⚡ 1. Electrostatic Potential

The work done by an external force in bringing a unit positive charge from infinity to a point, without acceleration, against the electrostatic force.

V = W / q. Unit: Volt (V) or J/C.

Potential due to a point charge q: V = kq / r

💡 Relation between E and V

Electric field is the negative gradient of potential.

E = - dV / dr

This implies that electric field is always directed towards decreasing potential.

⭕ 2. Equipotential Surfaces

A surface with a constant value of potential at all points on the surface.

Properties:

  • No work is done in moving a test charge over an equipotential surface (W = qΔV = 0).
  • Electric field is always perpendicular to the equipotential surface at every point.
  • Two equipotential surfaces can never intersect each other.
  • For a point charge, the equipotential surfaces are concentric spheres.
Fig: Equipotential surfaces for a positive point charge

🔋 3. Electrostatic Potential Energy

The work done by an external force in assembling a system of charges from infinity to their present locations.

For a system of two charges: U = k(q1q2) / r

If the charges have the same sign, U is positive (repulsion). If they have opposite signs, U is negative (attraction).

Electrostatics of Conductors

  • Inside a conductor, electrostatic field is zero.
  • Interior of a conductor can have no excess charge.
  • Electrostatic potential is constant throughout the volume of the conductor and has the same value on its surface.

💡 4. Capacitance

Capacitance is the ability of a conductor to store electric charge and energy. Q = CV. Unit: Farad (F).

Parallel Plate Capacitor

C = ε0A / d

Where A = Area of plates, d = distance between them.

🚨 Exam Facts: Effect of Dielectric

When a dielectric slab of constant K is inserted between the plates, capacitance increases by K times.

C′ = K C0

  • Battery Disconnected: Q constant, V decreases (V/K), E decreases (E/K), U decreases (U/K).
  • Battery Connected: V constant, Q increases (KQ), E constant, U increases (KU).

🔗 5. Combination of Capacitors

💡 Formulas
  • Series Combination: Charge (Q) is same, Voltage divides.
    1/Ceq = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + ...
  • Parallel Combination: Voltage (V) is same, Charge divides.
    Ceq = C1 + C2 + ...

Energy Stored in a Capacitor

U = ½ CV2 = Q2 / 2C = ½ QV

💡 Short Trick: Common Potential

When two charged capacitors are connected, charge flows until their potentials become equal. Common Potential V = (C1V1 + C2V2) / (C1 + C2). Heat loss ΔH = ½ [C1C2/(C1+C2)] (V1-V2)2.
