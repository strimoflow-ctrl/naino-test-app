💧 1. Pressure and Pascal's Law

Pressure: The normal force exerted by a fluid per unit area. P = F/A. Unit: Pascal (Pa) or N/m².

Atmospheric Pressure: 1 atm = 1.013 × 10&sup5; Pa. Gauge pressure: P - Pa = ρgh.

Pascal's Law

A change in pressure applied to an enclosed incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and to the walls of its container.

Fig: Principle of Hydraulic Lift (Pascal's Law)

🌊 2. Archimedes' Principle

When a body is wholly or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.

Buoyant Force (Fb) = Vimmersed × ρfluid × g

Law of Floatation

A body will float in a liquid if the weight of the liquid displaced by the immersed part of the body is equal to the weight of the body. (i.e., ρbody ≤ ρfluid).

Fraction of volume immersed: Vin / Vtotal = ρbody / ρfluid.

💨 3. Fluid Dynamics

Equation of Continuity

Based on the conservation of mass. For an incompressible and non-viscous fluid flowing through a tube of varying cross-section, the product of cross-sectional area and fluid velocity is constant.

A1v1 = A2v2 (or Av = constant).

The product Av is the Volume flow rate.

Bernoulli's Principle

Based on the conservation of energy. It states that for a streamline flow of an ideal fluid, the sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy per unit volume remains constant.

💡 Bernoulli's Equation

P + ½ρv² + ρgh = Constant

Applications:

  • Torricelli's Law (Velocity of Efflux): v = √(2gh).
  • Venturimeter: Device used to measure the rate of flow of a liquid.
  • Aerofoil (Airplane Lift): Velocity is higher above the wing, so pressure is lower. Velocity is lower below the wing, so pressure is higher. This pressure difference creates an upward lift.

🍯 4. Viscosity

Viscosity is the internal friction in a fluid. It is the property by virtue of which a fluid opposes relative motion between its different layers.

Newton's Law of Viscous Force: F = -η A (dv/dx)
Where η is the coefficient of viscosity, A is the area of layers in contact, and dv/dx is the velocity gradient.

Stokes' Law & Terminal Velocity

When a spherical body of radius r falls through a viscous medium, the viscous drag force is given by Stokes' law: F = 6πηrv.

Terminal Velocity (vt): The maximum constant velocity acquired by the body when the net force on it becomes zero (Weight = Buoyant Force + Viscous Drag).

vt = [2r²(ρ - σ)g] / 9η

💧 5. Surface Tension

Surface tension (S) is the property of a liquid surface at rest to shrink into the minimum surface area possible. It acts tangentially to the liquid surface. S = Force / Length.

  • Surface Energy: Work done to increase the surface area of a liquid film by unity. W = S × ΔA.
  • Excess Pressure inside a Liquid Drop: Pex = 2S / R.
  • Excess Pressure inside a Soap Bubble: Pex = 4S / R (since soap bubble has two surfaces in contact with air).

Capillarity

The phenomenon of rise or fall of a liquid in a capillary tube.

💡 Ascent Formula

Height of capillary rise: h = 2S cosθ / (rρg)

Where θ is the angle of contact. If θ < 90° (acute), liquid rises (meniscus concave). If θ > 90° (obtuse), liquid falls (meniscus convex).
