Electric Charges and Fields

Class 12 Physics | Chapter 1
🎯 Target: NEET UG | Fully NCERT Based

⚡ 1. Electric Charge

Charge is a fundamental property of matter that causes it to experience a force when placed in an electromagnetic field. Types: Positive (due to loss of electrons) and Negative (due to gain of electrons).

Basic Properties of Charge:

  • Quantization: Charge on any body is an integral multiple of elementary charge (e).
    q = ±ne (where n is an integer, e = 1.6 × 10-19 C).
  • Conservation: Total charge of an isolated system remains constant.
  • Additivity: Total charge of a system is the algebraic sum of all individual charges located in the system.
  • Invariance: The magnitude of charge is independent of the velocity of the body.
🤯 Amazing Info: Quarks

Protons and neutrons are made up of smaller particles called Quarks, which have fractional charges like ±e/3 or ±2e/3. However, quarks do not exist in a free state, so the quantization law (integral multiple of e) remains valid macroscopically.

🧲 2. Coulomb's Law

The electrostatic force of interaction between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

💡 Formula

F = k (q1 q2) / r2

Where k = 1 / (4πε0) ≈ 9 × 109 N m2 C-2.

ε0 is the permittivity of free space = 8.854 × 10-12 C2 N-1 m-2.

Dielectric Constant (K or εr): If charges are placed in a medium, the force decreases. Fmedium = Fvacuum / K.

🌐 3. Electric Field

The region around a charge where its electrical influence can be felt by a test charge.

Electric Field Intensity (E): Force experienced by a unit positive test charge. E = F / q0. Unit: N/C or V/m.

Electric Field due to a point charge q at distance r: E = k q / r2.

Fig: Electric Field Lines for isolated point charges

Properties of Electric Field Lines:

  • They start from positive charge and end at negative charge.
  • Tangent to the field line at any point gives the direction of electric field at that point.
  • Two field lines can never intersect each other.
  • They never form closed loops (conservative nature of electric field).

↔️ 4. Electric Dipole

A system of two equal and opposite charges (+q and -q) separated by a small distance (2a).

Dipole Moment (p): p = q × 2a. It is a vector quantity directed from negative to positive charge.

Electric Field due to Dipole:

  • Axial point: Eaxial = 2kp / r3 (Direction: along p).
  • Equatorial point: Eeq = kp / r3 (Direction: opposite to p).
🚨 Exam Facts: Dipole in Uniform Field

Net Force = 0 (always).
Torque: τ = pE sinθ or τ = p × E.
Potential Energy: U = -pE cosθ or U = -p • E.

Stable equilibrium: θ = 0° (U = min). Unstable equilibrium: θ = 180° (U = max).

📦 5. Gauss's Law

Total electric flux (Φ) through any closed surface is equal to 1/ε0 times the net charge enclosed by the surface.

Φ = ∮ E • dA = qenclosed / ε0

Applications of Gauss's Law:

  • Infinitely long straight charged wire: E = λ / (2πε0r)
  • Infinite plane sheet of charge: E = σ / (2ε0) [Independent of distance r]
  • Thin spherical shell:
    • Outside (r > R): E = kq / r2
    • On surface (r = R): E = kq / R2
    • Inside (r < R): E = 0
💡 Short Trick: Cube Problem

If charge q is placed at:
Center of cube: Flux through one face = q / 6ε0
Corner of cube: Enclosed charge = q/8. Total flux = q / 8ε0. Flux through one non-touching face = q / 24ε0.
