📏 1. Distance and Displacement

Kinematics is the branch of mechanics which deals with the study of motion of objects without taking into account the cause of their motion.

  • Distance: Total length of the path traversed by a particle in a given time interval. It is a scalar quantity. Always positive.
  • Displacement: Shortest distance between initial and final position of the particle. It is a vector quantity. Can be positive, negative or zero.
  • For a moving body: Distance ≥ |Displacement|
🚨 Exam Facts: Displacement zero?

If a particle starts from a point and returns to the same point after some time, its displacement is zero, but the distance travelled is NOT zero.

⏱️ 2. Speed and Velocity

Speed

Rate of distance covered with time. Scalar quantity.

  • Average Speed: Total Distance / Total Time.
  • Instantaneous Speed: Speed at a particular instant of time. (v = ds/dt)

Velocity

Rate of change of position (displacement) with time. Vector quantity.

  • Average Velocity: Total Displacement / Total Time.
  • Instantaneous Velocity: Velocity at a specific instant. (v = dx/dt)
  • For a moving body: Average Speed ≥ |Average Velocity|
💡 Key Formula: Average Speed in equal distances

If a car covers distance d with speed v1 and another equal distance d with speed v2, its average speed is the harmonic mean:
Vavg = (2 × v1 × v2) / (v1 + v2)

🚀 3. Acceleration

The rate of change of velocity with respect to time is called acceleration. It is a vector quantity.

  • Average Acceleration: aavg = Δv / Δt = (v2 - v1) / (t2 - t1)
  • Instantaneous Acceleration: a = dv/dt = v(dv/dx) = d2x/dt2
  • If velocity and acceleration are in the same direction, speed increases. If they are in opposite directions (retardation), speed decreases.

📈 4. Kinematic Graphs

1. Position-Time (x-t) Graph

Slope of x-t graph gives velocity. If x-t graph is a straight line, velocity is constant (zero acceleration). If it's a parabola, velocity is changing (constant acceleration).

2. Velocity-Time (v-t) Graph

Slope of v-t graph gives acceleration. Area under v-t graph gives displacement (with sign) and area without sign gives distance.

3. Acceleration-Time (a-t) Graph

Area under a-t graph gives change in velocity (Δv = v - u).

➗ 5. Equations of Motion

For a particle moving with uniform acceleration 'a' in a straight line:

💡 Kinematic Equations
  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at2
  • v2 = u2 + 2as
  • Displacement in nth second: Sn = u + (a/2)(2n - 1)

Motion Under Gravity

When an object is thrown upwards or dropped, it moves under uniform acceleration g (acceleration due to gravity, roughly 9.8 m/s2).

  • Taking downward direction as negative: a = -g.
  • For an object dropped from rest: u = 0, a = g (if downward is positive). h = ½gt2.
  • For an object thrown vertically upwards with velocity u, max height reached is Hmax = u2 / 2g and time of ascent is T = u / g.
