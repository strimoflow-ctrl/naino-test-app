🌊 1. Displacement Current

Maxwell found an inconsistency in Ampere's circuital law when applying it to a charging capacitor. To resolve this, he introduced the concept of displacement current (Id).

Displacement current is that current which comes into existence (in addition to conduction current) whenever the electric field and hence the electric flux changes with time.

💡 Maxwell's modification

Id = ε0 (dΦE / dt)

Modified Ampere-Maxwell Law: ∮ B • dl = μ0 (Ic + Id)

⚡ 2. Characteristics of EM Waves

Accelerated charges produce electromagnetic waves. An oscillating charge produces an oscillating electric field in space, which produces an oscillating magnetic field, which in turn is a source of oscillating electric field, and so on. They regenerate each other as a wave.

  • In EM waves, the electric field (E) and magnetic field (B) are mutually perpendicular to each other and also perpendicular to the direction of propagation. (Transverse nature).
  • The direction of propagation is given by E × B.
  • In free space, speed c = 1 / √(μ0ε0) ≈ 3 × 108 m/s.
  • The magnitudes of E and B are related as: c = E0 / B0 = E / B.
  • They do not require any material medium for propagation.

🔋 3. Energy and Momentum of EM Waves

EM waves carry both energy and momentum, which they share equally between the electric and magnetic fields.

Average Energy Density (u) = ½ ε0Erms2 + ½ (Brms2/μ0)

Since energy is shared equally, uE = uB. Total u = ε0Erms2 = Brms2/μ0.

🚨 Exam Facts: Radiation Pressure

Because EM waves carry momentum, they exert pressure on surfaces they strike, called Radiation Pressure (p).

  • If the surface is perfectly absorbing: Momentum p = U / c.
  • If the surface is perfectly reflecting: Momentum p = 2U / c.

🌈 4. Electromagnetic Spectrum

The orderly arrangement of EM waves according to their wavelength or frequency is called the electromagnetic spectrum.

💡 Trick to remember order (Low Frequency to High Frequency)

Radio, Microwaves, Infrared, Visible, Ultraviolet, X-rays, Gamma rays.
