🌊 1. Types of Waves

A wave is a disturbance that travels through a medium, transferring energy and momentum from one point to another without the transport of matter.

  • Transverse Waves: Particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. (e.g., Light waves, waves on a string). Consist of crests and troughs. Can be polarized.
  • Longitudinal Waves: Particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation. (e.g., Sound waves). Consist of compressions and rarefactions. Cannot be polarized.
📘 Equation of a Progressive Wave

y(x,t) = A sin(ωt - kx + φ) (Wave travelling in +x direction)

Where:
A = Amplitude
ω = Angular frequency (2πf)
k = Angular wave number (2π/λ)
Wave velocity (v) = ω/k = fλ

🏎️ 2. Speed of a Traveling Wave

Speed of a Transverse Wave on a Stretched String

v = √(T / μ)

Where T is tension in the string and μ is linear mass density (mass per unit length).

Speed of a Longitudinal Wave (Sound)

v = √(B / ρ) (Newton's formula, B = Bulk modulus).

Newton assumed sound propagation is an isothermal process, which gave a wrong result for speed of sound in air. Laplace corrected it by stating that it is an adiabatic process.

Laplace Correction: v = √(γP / ρ)

🚨 Exam Facts: Factors affecting speed of sound in air
  • Temperature: Speed increases with temperature. (v ∝ √T).
  • Humidity: Speed of sound is greater in moist air than in dry air (because density of moist air is less than dry air).
  • Pressure: Speed of sound is independent of pressure (provided temperature remains constant).

🎵 3. Superposition of Waves

When two or more waves travel through the same medium simultaneously, the resultant displacement at any point is the vector sum of the displacements of the individual waves. y = y1 + y2.

Interference

Modification in the distribution of energy due to superposition of two waves of same frequency.

  • Constructive Interference: Phase difference Δφ = 2nπ. Path difference Δx = nλ. Intensity is maximum.
  • Destructive Interference: Phase difference Δφ = (2n+1)π. Path difference Δx = (2n+1)λ/2. Intensity is minimum.

🎻 4. Standing Waves & Beats

Standing (Stationary) Waves

Formed by superposition of two identical waves travelling in opposite directions. They do not transfer energy. Nodes (N) are points of zero amplitude. Antinodes (A) are points of maximum amplitude.

💡 Organ Pipes
  • Open Organ Pipe: Both ends are open (Antinodes at both ends). Produces both odd and even harmonics. Fundamental frequency f1 = v / 2L. Ratio = 1 : 2 : 3...
  • Closed Organ Pipe: One end closed (Node), other open (Antinode). Produces ONLY odd harmonics. Fundamental frequency f1 = v / 4L. Ratio = 1 : 3 : 5...

Beats

Periodic variations in the intensity of sound due to the superposition of two sound waves of slightly different frequencies.

Beat Frequency (fb) = |f1 - f2|

🚑 5. Doppler Effect in Sound

The apparent change in the frequency of sound due to relative motion between the source and the observer.

Apparent frequency (f′):

f′ = f [ (v ± vo) / (v ∓ vs) ]

Where:
v = speed of sound in the medium
vo = speed of observer (+ if moving towards source, - if moving away)
vs = speed of source (- if moving towards observer, + if moving away)

💡 Short Trick: Sign Convention

If distance between source and observer is decreasing, apparent frequency must increase (numerator larger, denominator smaller).
If distance is increasing, apparent frequency must decrease.
