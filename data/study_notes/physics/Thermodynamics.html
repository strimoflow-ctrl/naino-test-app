🔥 1. Zeroth and First Law of Thermodynamics

Zeroth Law

If two systems A and B are in thermal equilibrium with a third system C separately, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This law leads to the concept of Temperature.

First Law of Thermodynamics

It is essentially the law of conservation of energy applied to a thermodynamic system.

💡 First Law Equation

ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW

Where ΔQ is heat supplied to the system, ΔU is change in internal energy, and ΔW is work done by the system.

Sign Convention:

  • ΔQ is positive if heat is supplied to the system.
  • ΔW is positive if work is done by the system (expansion).
  • ΔU is positive if temperature increases.

🔄 2. Thermodynamic Processes

Fig: P-V Curves for different processes starting from same initial state
  • Isothermal: Temperature is constant (ΔT = 0, ΔU = 0). Equation: PV = constant. Work done: W = nRT ln(V2/V1).
  • Adiabatic: No heat exchange (ΔQ = 0). Equation: PVγ = constant. Work done: W = (P1V1 - P2V2) / (γ - 1).
  • Isochoric: Volume is constant (ΔV = 0, ΔW = 0). Therefore, ΔQ = ΔU.
  • Isobaric: Pressure is constant (ΔP = 0). Work done: W = P(V2 - V1).

🌡️ 3. Specific Heat Capacity of Gases

Gases have two principal specific heats:

  • Cp: Molar specific heat at constant pressure.
  • Cv: Molar specific heat at constant volume.

Mayer's Formula: Cp - Cv = R (Universal Gas Constant).

Ratio of specific heats (γ): γ = Cp / Cv.

🚨 Exam Facts: Value of γ

Monatomic gases (He, Ar): γ = 5/3 = 1.67

Diatomic gases (O2, N2): γ = 7/5 = 1.40

Polyatomic gases (CO2): γ = 4/3 = 1.33

⚙️ 4. Second Law of Thermodynamics

The First Law does not restrict the direction of heat flow. The Second Law provides this restriction.

  • Kelvin-Planck Statement: No process is possible whose sole result is the absorption of heat from a reservoir and the complete conversion of the heat into work. (Efficiency of a heat engine < 100%).
  • Clausius Statement: No process is possible whose sole result is the transfer of heat from a colder object to a hotter object. (Refrigerators need external work).

🚀 5. Heat Engines and Refrigerators

Heat Engine

A device which converts heat energy into mechanical work in a cyclic process. It takes heat Q1 from source at T1, does work W, and rejects heat Q2 to sink at T2.

Efficiency (η): η = W / Q1 = (Q1 - Q2) / Q1 = 1 - (Q2/Q1).

Carnot Engine

A theoretical ideal reversible heat engine with maximum possible efficiency.

Carnot Efficiency: η = 1 - (T2 / T1), where temperatures MUST be in Kelvin.

📘 Refrigerator (Heat Pump)

It is a heat engine working in reverse. It extracts heat Q2 from cold reservoir, work W is done on it, and it rejects heat Q1 to hot reservoir.

Coefficient of Performance (β): β = Q2 / W = Q2 / (Q1 - Q2) = T2 / (T1 - T2).
