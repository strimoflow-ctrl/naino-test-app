⏱️ 1. Periodic and Oscillatory Motion

  • Periodic Motion: A motion that repeats itself at regular intervals of time. (e.g., Earth revolving around the Sun).
  • Oscillatory Motion: A periodic motion in which a body moves to and fro about a fixed point (mean position). (e.g., Simple Pendulum).

Note: All oscillatory motions are periodic, but all periodic motions are not oscillatory.

📘 Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

SHM is the simplest form of oscillatory motion. In SHM, the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement from the mean position and is always directed towards the mean position.

F = -kx ⇒ a = -ω2x

🌊 2. Equations of SHM

The displacement of a particle executing SHM at any instant t is given by:

x(t) = A sin(ωt + φ)

Where:
A = Amplitude (maximum displacement)
ω = Angular frequency = 2π/T = √(k/m)
φ = Initial phase or epoch (depends on initial position and velocity).

Velocity and Acceleration

  • Velocity (v): v = dx/dt = Aω cos(ωt + φ).
    In terms of displacement: v = ω√(A2 - x2).
    Velocity is maximum at mean position (x = 0): vmax = Aω.
    Velocity is zero at extreme positions (x = ±A).
  • Acceleration (a): a = dv/dt = -Aω2 sin(ωt + φ).
    In terms of displacement: a = -ω2x.
    Acceleration is zero at mean position.
    Acceleration is maximum at extreme positions: amax = ω2A.

⚡ 3. Energy in SHM

A particle executing SHM possesses both kinetic and potential energy.

  • Kinetic Energy (K): K = ½mv2 = ½mω2(A2 - x2) = ½k(A2 - x2).
  • Potential Energy (U): U = ½kx2 = ½mω2x2.
  • Total Energy (E): E = K + U = ½kA2 = ½mω2A2.

Total energy in SHM remains constant and is proportional to the square of the amplitude.

🚨 Exam Facts: Frequency of Energy

If the frequency of a particle executing SHM is f, then the frequency of oscillation of its Kinetic Energy and Potential Energy is 2f. (Because they complete two cycles for every one cycle of displacement).

🕰️ 4. Systems Executing SHM

Simple Pendulum

A heavy point mass suspended by a weightless, inextensible, and perfectly flexible string.

Time Period: T = 2π √(L/g)

Note: Time period is independent of the mass of the bob and the amplitude (for small angular displacements).

Spring-Mass System

Time Period: T = 2π √(m/k)

💡 Combination of Springs
  • Series: 1/keq = 1/k1 + 1/k2
  • Parallel: keq = k1 + k2

🔉 5. Damped and Forced Oscillations

  • Damped Oscillations: The amplitude of oscillation decreases exponentially with time due to dissipative forces like friction or viscous drag. E(t) = E0 e-bt/m.
  • Forced Oscillations: The body is maintained in oscillation by an external periodic force. The body oscillates with the frequency of the external force, not its natural frequency.
  • Resonance: A special case of forced oscillation where the frequency of the external periodic force matches the natural frequency of the body. The amplitude of oscillation becomes very large.
