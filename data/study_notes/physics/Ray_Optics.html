🪞 1. Reflection of Light

The bouncing back of light into the same medium when it strikes a surface. Laws of Reflection: Angle of incidence (i) = Angle of reflection (r). The incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane.

Spherical Mirrors

  • Concave Mirror: Converging mirror. Real focus. Can form both real and virtual images.
  • Convex Mirror: Diverging mirror. Virtual focus. Always forms virtual, erect, and diminished images. (Used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles).
💡 Mirror Formula & Magnification

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Linear Magnification: m = h′ / h = -v / u

Note: Focus f = R / 2. Sign convention is strictly required: distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive.

🌊 2. Refraction of Light

The bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another due to a change in the speed of light.

Snell's Law: μ1 sin(i) = μ2 sin(r).
Refractive Index: μ = c / v.

Apparent Depth

A coin in a tank of water appears raised. Apparent depth = Real depth / μ.

Fig: Total Internal Reflection (TIR) from denser to rarer medium

Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

When light travels from denser to rarer medium and the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle (C), it is completely reflected back into the denser medium.

Critical Angle: sin(C) = 1 / μ

Applications: Optical fibers, mirage in deserts, brilliance of diamond.

🔍 3. Lenses

Lens Maker's Formula:
1/f = (μ - 1) [1/R1 - 1/R2]

Thin Lens Formula:
1/f = 1/v - 1/u

Power of a Lens (P): P = 1 / f(in meters). Unit: Diopter (D). Convex lens has positive power, concave has negative.

🚨 Exam Facts: Lens immersed in liquid

If a lens of refractive index μg is immersed in a liquid of refractive index μl:

  • If μl < μg (e.g., glass in water): Focal length increases, power decreases, but nature remains the same.
  • If μl = μg: Lens becomes invisible, f = infinity (acts as a plane glass plate).
  • If μl > μg (e.g., glass in CS2): Nature of lens reverses (convex acts as concave).

🌈 4. Prism

When light passes through a prism, it suffers refraction at two faces. The angle of deviation (δ) depends on angle of incidence, angle of prism (A), and refractive index.

δ = i + e - A

At minimum deviation (δm): i = e, and r1 = r2 = A/2. The refracted ray inside the prism is parallel to its base.

Prism Formula: μ = sin[(A + δm)/2] / sin(A/2)

Dispersion: The splitting of white light into its constituent colors (VIBGYOR). Violet deviates the most, Red deviates the least.

🔬 5. Optical Instruments

  • Simple Microscope (Magnifying Glass): A convex lens of short focal length. Magnifying power M = 1 + D/f (image at near point) or M = D/f (image at infinity).
  • Compound Microscope: Uses objective and eyepiece lenses. M = mo × me. M ≈ (L/fo) × (D/fe).
  • Astronomical Telescope: Used for viewing distant heavenly bodies. M = fo / fe (in normal adjustment). To increase M, fo should be large and fe small.
