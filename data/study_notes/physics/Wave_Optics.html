🌊 1. Huygens Principle

Light travels in the form of waves. The locus of all particles of the medium vibrating in the same phase is called a Wavefront.

  • Point source produces Spherical Wavefront.
  • Line source produces Cylindrical Wavefront.
  • Source at infinity produces Plane Wavefront.
📘 Huygens Principle

Every point on a given wavefront acts as a fresh source of new disturbance, called secondary wavelets, which travel in all directions with the velocity of light in the medium. The forward envelope of these wavelets gives the new wavefront at any later instant.

🔆 2. Interference of Light

The phenomenon of redistribution of light energy due to the superposition of light waves from two coherent sources.

Coherent Sources: Sources which emit light waves of same frequency and have a zero or constant phase difference. (Two independent bulbs can NEVER be coherent).

Conditions for Interference:

Resultant Intensity: I = I1 + I2 + 2√(I1I2) cosφ

  • Constructive (Bright fringe): φ = 0, 2π, 4π... or path diff = nλ. Imax = (√I1 + √I2)2.
  • Destructive (Dark fringe): φ = π, 3π... or path diff = (2n-1)λ/2. Imin = (√I1 - √I2)2.

🔬 3. Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE)

Demonstrated the wave nature of light. An interference pattern of alternate bright and dark fringes is formed on the screen.

Fig: Schematic of Young's Double Slit Experiment
💡 Fringe Width (β)

The distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes.

β = λ D / d

If the entire YDSE apparatus is immersed in water of refractive index μ, wavelength becomes λ/μ. Thus, the fringe width decreases (β′ = β/μ).

🌈 4. Diffraction of Light

The phenomenon of bending of light around the corners of an obstacle or aperture and its encroachment into the region of geometrical shadow. It becomes prominent when the size of the obstacle is comparable to the wavelength of light.

Single Slit Diffraction

A central bright maximum is formed, surrounded by alternate secondary minima and maxima on both sides.

  • Condition for Minima (Dark): a sinθ = nλ (n = 1, 2, 3...)
  • Condition for Maxima (Bright): a sinθ = (2n+1)λ/2

Width of Central Maximum: W = 2λD / a. It is twice as wide as the secondary maxima.

🚨 Exam Facts: Interference vs Diffraction

In Interference, all bright fringes have the same intensity. In Diffraction, the intensity of secondary maxima decreases rapidly as we move away from the central maximum.

🕶️ 5. Polarization

The phenomenon of restricting the vibrations of light waves in a single plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation. This proves that light waves are transverse in nature.

  • Malus's Law: When completely plane-polarized light is incident on an analyzer, the intensity of emergent light varies as: I = I0 cos2θ. (If unpolarized light of intensity I0 falls on a polarizer, transmitted intensity is always I0/2).
  • Brewster's Law: At a particular angle of incidence called polarizing angle (ip), the reflected light is completely plane-polarized. μ = tan(ip). At this angle, the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular to each other.
