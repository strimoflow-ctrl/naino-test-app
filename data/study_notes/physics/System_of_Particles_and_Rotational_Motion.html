🎯 1. Centre of Mass

The centre of mass of a system of particles is the point where the entire mass of the system can be supposed to be concentrated for describing its translational motion.

Position Vector of COM: RCM = (m1r1 + m2r2 + ... + mnrn) / (m1 + m2 + ... + mn)

If no external force acts on a system (Fext = 0), the velocity of its centre of mass remains constant. The internal forces do not affect the motion of the centre of mass.

🚨 Exam Facts: Exploding Bomb

If a bomb explodes mid-air, it breaks into many pieces due to internal forces. Since external force is zero, the Centre of Mass of all the fragments continues to move in the original parabolic path.

🔄 2. Torque and Angular Momentum

Torque (τ)

Torque is the rotational analogue of force. It measures the turning effect of a force.
τ = r × F = r F sinθ n

Torque is maximum when force is perpendicular to the position vector (θ = 90°).

Angular Momentum (L)

Rotational analogue of linear momentum.
L = r × p = r p sinθ n

Relation: Just as F = dp/dt, we have τ = dL/dt.

If net external torque is zero (τ = 0), then Angular Momentum is Conserved (L = constant).

⚙️ 3. Moment of Inertia (I)

Moment of inertia is the rotational analogue of mass. It represents the inertia of a rotating body. It depends on mass, its distribution about the axis, and the position of the axis of rotation.

I = Σ mi ri2

Radius of Gyration (k): It is the distance from the axis of rotation where the entire mass of the body can be assumed to be concentrated. I = mk2.

📏 4. Theorems of Moment of Inertia

Fig: Theorems of Moment of Inertia

1. Parallel Axis Theorem

I = ICM + Md2 (Applicable to 1D, 2D, and 3D bodies).

2. Perpendicular Axis Theorem

Iz = Ix + Iy (Applicable ONLY to 2D planar bodies/lamina). The x and y axes must lie in the plane of the body and intersect at the origin of the z-axis.

🌀 5. Rolling Motion

Pure rolling (rolling without slipping) is a combination of translational and rotational motion.

Condition for pure rolling: vCM = Rω

Kinetic Energy of Rolling Body: K = Ktrans + Krot = ½mv2 + ½Iω2 = ½mv2 [1 + (k2/R2)]

💡 Short Trick: Rolling down an incline

When bodies of same mass and radius roll down an incline, the one with the smallest I reaches the bottom first.
Order of reaching bottom: Solid Sphere > Disc > Solid Cylinder > Hollow Sphere > Ring.
