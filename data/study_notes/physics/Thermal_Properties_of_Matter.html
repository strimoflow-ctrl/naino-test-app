🌡️ 1. Temperature and Heat

Heat is the form of energy transferred between two systems or a system and its surroundings by virtue of temperature difference. Unit: Joule (J) or Calorie (cal). 1 cal = 4.186 J.

Temperature is the degree of hotness or coldness of a body. It determines the direction of heat flow.

Temperature Scales

Relation between different temperature scales:

(C - 0)/100 = (F - 32)/180 = (K - 273.15)/100

🚨 Exam Facts: Unique Points
  • At -40°, the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales show the same reading (-40°C = -40°F).
  • Absolute zero is 0 K or -273.15°C. It is the lowest possible temperature.

📏 2. Thermal Expansion

Increase in the dimensions of a body due to an increase in its temperature is called thermal expansion.

  • Linear Expansion: ΔL = α L ΔT (for 1D, like wires).
  • Areal/Superficial Expansion: ΔA = β A ΔT (for 2D, like sheets).
  • Volume Expansion: ΔV = γ V ΔT (for 3D, like blocks).
💡 Relation between α, β, γ

For isotropic solids: α : β : γ = 1 : 2 : 3.
Hence, β = 2α and γ = 3α.

Anomalous Expansion of Water

Water exhibits anomalous behavior: it contracts on heating between 0°C and 4°C. Thus, density of water is maximum at 4°C. This helps aquatic life survive in frozen lakes.

🔥 3. Calorimetry

Calorimetry is the measurement of heat. Principle: Heat lost by hot body = Heat gained by cold body.

Specific Heat Capacity (s or c)

Amount of heat required to raise the temperature of unit mass of a substance by 1°C.
Q = mcΔT

Latent Heat (L)

Amount of heat required to change the state of unit mass of a substance at constant temperature.
Q = mL

  • Latent heat of fusion: Solid to Liquid (e.g., Ice to Water). For ice, Lf ≈ 80 cal/g.
  • Latent heat of vaporization: Liquid to Gas (e.g., Water to Steam). For water, Lv ≈ 540 cal/g.

🌊 4. Heat Transfer

Heat can be transferred from one place to another by three modes:

  1. Conduction: Transfer of heat through material medium without actual movement of particles. Governed by Fourier's Law: dQ/dt = -KA(dT/dx), where K is thermal conductivity.
  2. Convection: Transfer of heat by actual bodily movement of the heated fluid particles. (e.g., boiling water, sea breeze).
  3. Radiation: Transfer of heat in the form of electromagnetic waves without requiring any material medium.
📘 Thermal Resistance (R)

Similar to electrical resistance, thermal resistance R = L / KA. Heat current H = ΔT / R.

☀️ 5. Radiation Laws

  • Stefan-Boltzmann Law: The total radiant energy emitted per second per unit surface area of a perfectly black body is proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.
    E = σT4 (where σ is Stefan's constant).
  • Wien's Displacement Law: The wavelength (λm) corresponding to maximum energy emission by a black body is inversely proportional to its absolute temperature.
    λmT = b (where b is Wien's constant).
  • Newton's Law of Cooling: The rate of cooling of a body is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings (for small temperature differences).
    -dT/dt = k(T - T0)
