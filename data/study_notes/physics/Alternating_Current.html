⚡ 1. RMS and Mean Values of AC

An alternating current changes its magnitude continuously and reverses its direction periodically. I = I0 sin(ωt).

  • Mean (Average) Value: Over one complete cycle, Iavg = 0.
    Over half cycle, Iavg = 2I0 / π ≈ 0.637 I0.
  • RMS (Root Mean Square) Value: The value of steady current which would generate the same heat in a resistor as is generated by the AC in the same time.
    Irms = I0 / √2 ≈ 0.707 I0.

Note: All AC meters measure RMS values. The household supply 220V refers to the RMS voltage. The peak voltage is 220√2 ≈ 311V, which is why AC is more dangerous than DC of the same voltage.

🔌 2. AC Circuits with R, L, and C

💡 Reactance & Phase Difference
Circuit Component Reactance / Resistance Phase of Current w.r.t Voltage
Resistor (R) R In phase (φ = 0)
Inductor (L) XL = ωL Current Lags by 90° (π/2)
Capacitor (C) XC = 1 / ωC Current Leads by 90° (π/2)
💡 Trick: ELI the ICE man

E L I: In an Inductor (L), Voltage (E) leads Current (I).
I C E: In a Capacitor (C), Current (I) leads Voltage (E).

📻 3. Series LCR Circuit and Resonance

When L, C, and R are connected in series to an AC source:

Impedance (Z): Z = √[R2 + (XL - XC)2]

Phase angle (φ): tanφ = (XL - XC) / R

Electrical Resonance

Occurs when XL = XC. In this condition:

  • Impedance Z is minimum (Z = R).
  • Current I is maximum (Imax = V/R).
  • Resonant frequency: fr = 1 / (2π√LC).
  • Voltage and Current are in phase (φ = 0).

Quality Factor (Q-factor): Determines the sharpness of tuning at resonance. Q = (1/R) √(L/C).

🔋 4. Power in AC Circuits

The average power dissipated in an AC circuit over a complete cycle is:

Pavg = Vrms Irms cosφ

Where cosφ is called the Power Factor. cosφ = R / Z.

🚨 Exam Facts: Wattless Current

For purely inductive or purely capacitive circuits, φ = 90°, so cosφ = 0. Therefore, Pavg = 0.
The current in such a circuit is called Wattless Current (Idle current) because it dissipates no average power.

⚡ 5. Transformers

A device used to increase (step-up) or decrease (step-down) the alternating voltage based on Mutual Induction.

Transformation Ratio (k):
Vs / Vp = Ns / Np = Ip / Is = k

  • Step-Up Transformer: Ns > Np. Voltage increases, current decreases.
  • Step-Down Transformer: Ns < Np. Voltage decreases, current increases.

Energy Losses: Copper loss (Joule heating), Iron loss (Eddy currents - minimized by laminated core), Hysteresis loss, Flux leakage.
