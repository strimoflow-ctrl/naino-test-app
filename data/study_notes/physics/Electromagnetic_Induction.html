🧲 1. Magnetic Flux and Faraday's Law

Magnetic Flux (Φ): The total number of magnetic field lines crossing normally through a surface area. Φ = B • A = BA cosθ. Unit: Weber (Wb).

Faraday's Laws of EMI

  • First Law: Whenever there is a change in the magnetic flux linked with a circuit, an induced electromotive force (EMF) is produced.
  • Second Law: The magnitude of the induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.
💡 Formula

e = - N (dΦ / dt)

Where N is the number of turns. The negative sign is explained by Lenz's Law.

↩️ 2. Lenz's Law

The direction of the induced EMF or current is always such as to oppose the change that produces it. It is a direct consequence of the Law of Conservation of Energy.

Fig: Illustration of Lenz's Law (Opposing the cause)

🚄 3. Motional EMF

The EMF induced across the ends of a conductor due to its motion in a magnetic field.

If a straight rod of length l moves with velocity v perpendicular to uniform magnetic field B, the motional EMF is:

e = B l v

🚨 Exam Facts: Rotating Rod

If a metallic rod of length L is rotated with angular velocity ω in a uniform magnetic field B perpendicular to the plane of rotation, the EMF induced between its ends is:

e = ½ B L2 ω

🔄 4. Self and Mutual Induction

Self Induction (L)

The phenomenon of production of induced EMF in a coil due to a change in current flowing through the same coil.

Φ = L I ⇒ e = -L (dI/dt). Unit of L: Henry (H).

Self Inductance of a long solenoid: L = μ0 n2 A l

Mutual Induction (M)

The phenomenon of production of induced EMF in one coil due to a change in current in a neighboring coil.

Φ2 = M I1 ⇒ e2 = -M (dI1/dt)

📘 Energy stored in an Inductor

Energy stored in the magnetic field of an inductor: U = ½ L I2.

🔌 5. AC Generator

Based on the principle of electromagnetic induction. It converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.

A coil of N turns and area A is rotated with angular velocity ω in a uniform magnetic field B.

Flux at time t: Φ = NBA cos(ωt)
Induced EMF: e = -dΦ/dt = NBAω sin(ωt)

Peak EMF: e0 = NBAω. Hence, e = e0 sin(ωt).

Eddy Currents: Circulating currents induced in bulk pieces of metal when subjected to changing magnetic flux. They cause heating (can be minimized by using laminated cores).
