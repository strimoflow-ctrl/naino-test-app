Kinetic Theory of Gases

Class 11 Physics | Chapter 12
🎯 Target: NEET UG | Fully NCERT Based

💨 1. Assumptions of Kinetic Theory

The kinetic theory of gases models a gas as a large number of very small particles (atoms or molecules) which are in constant, random motion.

  • Gas consists of large number of identical, tiny spherical molecules.
  • Molecules are in constant random motion, colliding elastically with each other and the walls of the container.
  • The volume of molecules is negligible compared to the volume of the container.
  • There are no intermolecular forces of attraction or repulsion between the molecules (for ideal gases).
  • The time of collision is negligible compared to the time between collisions.

📏 2. Ideal Gas Equation

A gas that strictly obeys all gas laws at all temperatures and pressures is called an ideal gas.

💡 Ideal Gas Law

PV = nRT = NkT

Where:
P = Pressure, V = Volume, T = Absolute Temperature (in K)
n = Number of moles, N = Number of molecules
R = Universal Gas Constant (8.314 J/mol·K)
k = Boltzmann's Constant (1.38 × 10-23 J/K). Note: k = R / NA.

Real gases behave like ideal gases at High Temperature and Low Pressure.

💥 3. Pressure of an Ideal Gas

The pressure exerted by a gas is due to the continuous bombardment of gas molecules against the walls of the container.

P = (1/3) ρ vrms2

Where ρ is density of gas and vrms is the root mean square velocity.

Different Velocities of Gas Molecules

  • Root Mean Square Velocity (vrms): √(3RT/M) = √(3kT/m)
  • Average Velocity (vavg): √(8RT/πM)
  • Most Probable Velocity (vmp): √(2RT/M)
💡 Trick to remember order: RAM

Order of velocities: Rms > Average > Most Probable.
vrms : vavg : vmp = √3 : √(8/π) : √2

🌡️ 4. Kinetic Interpretation of Temperature

The average translational kinetic energy of a molecule is directly proportional to the absolute temperature of the gas.

Etrans = (3/2) kT (per molecule)
Etrans = (3/2) RT (per mole)

This shows that at absolute zero (T = 0 K), the kinetic energy of gas molecules becomes zero, meaning molecular motion stops.

⚖️ 5. Degrees of Freedom & Equipartition of Energy

Degrees of Freedom (f)

The number of independent ways in which a dynamic system can move without violating any constraint.

  • Monoatomic (e.g., He, Ne): f = 3 (only translational).
  • Diatomic (e.g., O2, N2): f = 5 (3 translational + 2 rotational) at room temp.
  • Polyatomic (e.g., CO2, NH3): f = 6 (3 trans + 3 rot).

Law of Equipartition of Energy

In thermal equilibrium, the total energy of the system is shared equally among all its active degrees of freedom, and the average energy associated with each degree of freedom per molecule is ½ kT.

Total Internal Energy (U) of 1 mole of gas = (f/2) RT.

🚨 Exam Facts: Mean Free Path (λ)

The average distance covered by a molecule between two successive collisions.

λ = 1 / (√2 π n d2)

Where n = number density (N/V), and d = diameter of molecule. Note: λ is inversely proportional to pressure and directly proportional to temperature.
