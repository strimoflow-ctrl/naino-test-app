🔌 1. Energy Bands and Semiconductors

In solids, electrons are restricted to bands of energy. The highest filled band is the Valence Band (VB) and the lowest empty/partially filled band is the Conduction Band (CB). The gap between them is the Forbidden Energy Gap (Eg).

  • Conductors: VB and CB overlap. Eg = 0. Lots of free electrons.
  • Insulators: Large energy gap (Eg > 3 eV). No free electrons at room temperature.
  • Semiconductors: Small energy gap (Eg < 3 eV). E.g., Silicon (1.1 eV) and Germanium (0.72 eV). At 0K, behaves as insulator. At room temp, some electrons jump to CB leaving 'holes' in VB.

⚛️ 2. Types of Semiconductors

Intrinsic Semiconductors

Pure semiconductors (Si, Ge). Number of electrons (ne) = Number of holes (nh) = ni.

Extrinsic Semiconductors

Impure semiconductors formed by doping to increase conductivity.

  • n-type: Doped with Pentavalent impurity (P, As, Sb). Electrons are majority carriers (ne >> nh). Donor energy level is just below CB.
  • p-type: Doped with Trivalent impurity (B, Al, In). Holes are majority carriers (nh >> ne). Acceptor energy level is just above VB.
💡 Mass Action Law

ne × nh = ni2 (Valid for both intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors in thermal equilibrium).

🔋 3. p-n Junction Diode

When a p-type semiconductor is suitably joined to an n-type semiconductor, a p-n junction is formed.

  • Depletion Region: A thin layer near the junction devoid of charge carriers, containing immobile positive (n-side) and negative (p-side) ions.
  • Forward Bias: p connected to positive, n to negative terminal. Depletion width decreases, resistance is low, current flows easily.
  • Reverse Bias: p connected to negative, n to positive. Depletion width increases, resistance is very high, almost zero current flows (except tiny leakage current due to minority carriers).

🛠️ 4. Applications of Diode

Because a diode allows current only in one direction, it is used as a Rectifier (converts AC to DC).

  • Half-Wave Rectifier: Uses 1 diode. Conducts only during positive half cycle. Efficiency ≈ 40.6%. Ripple frequency = fin.
  • Full-Wave Rectifier: Uses 2 diodes (with center-tap transformer) or 4 diodes (bridge). Conducts during both cycles. Efficiency ≈ 81.2%. Ripple frequency = 2fin.

Special Purpose Diodes

  • Zener Diode: Highly doped, operates in reverse breakdown region. Used as a Voltage Regulator.
  • Photodiode: Operates in reverse bias. Used to detect light signals.
  • LED (Light Emitting Diode): Operates in forward bias. Converts electrical energy into light energy.
  • Solar Cell: A p-n junction that generates EMF when solar radiation falls on it. No external bias applied.

🧮 5. Logic Gates

Digital circuits that follow certain logical relationships between input and output voltages.

🚨 Exam Facts: Truth Tables
  • NOT Gate: Inverter. Y = A′. (Output is opposite of input).
  • OR Gate: Addition. Y = A + B. (High if ANY input is high).
  • AND Gate: Multiplication. Y = A • B. (High only if ALL inputs are high).
  • NAND Gate: Universal gate. Y = (A • B)′.
  • NOR Gate: Universal gate. Y = (A + B)′.
