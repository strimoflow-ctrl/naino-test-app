🔦 1. Photoelectric Effect

The phenomenon of emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of suitable frequency falls on it. The emitted electrons are called photoelectrons.

  • Work Function (Φ0 or W): Minimum energy required by an electron to escape from the metal surface. Unit: eV (1 eV = 1.6 × 10-19 J).
  • Threshold Frequency (ν0): Minimum frequency of incident light below which photoelectric emission is not possible, regardless of intensity.
  • Stopping Potential (V0): The minimum negative (retarding) potential given to the anode at which the photoelectric current becomes zero. Maximum Kinetic Energy Kmax = eV0.

⚖️ 2. Laws of Photoelectric Emission

Classical wave theory failed to explain the photoelectric effect. Einstein used Planck's quantum theory to explain it.

  1. For a given metal, there exists a certain minimum frequency (Threshold Frequency).
  2. The number of photoelectrons emitted per second is directly proportional to the Intensity of incident light (provided ν > ν0).
  3. The maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons is independent of intensity, but depends linearly on the Frequency of the incident light.
  4. The photoelectric emission is an instantaneous process (time lag ≤ 10-9 s).

🎯 3. Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

According to Einstein, light consists of packets of energy called photons. Energy of each photon E = hν.

When a photon hits a metal, its energy is used in two ways: freeing the electron (Work function) and giving it kinetic energy.

💡 Einstein's Equation

hν = Φ0 + Kmax

Or, Kmax = h(ν - ν0)

Also written as: eV0 = hc(1/λ - 1/λ0)

💡 Short Trick: Calculation of Energy

To find energy in eV directly from wavelength in Ångstroms (Å), use:
E (in eV) = 12400 / λ (in Å)

🌀 4. Matter Waves (De Broglie Hypothesis)

de Broglie proposed that just as radiation exhibits dual nature (wave and particle), matter also exhibits dual nature. A moving material particle is associated with a wave, called matter wave or de Broglie wave.

de Broglie Wavelength (λ):

λ = h / p = h / (mv) = h / √(2mK)

Where p is momentum and K is kinetic energy.

🚨 Exam Facts: Wavelength of an Accelerated Electron

If an electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference V volts, its de Broglie wavelength is:

λ = 12.27 / √V Å

This was experimentally verified by the Davisson-Germer experiment (which proved the wave nature of electrons through diffraction).
