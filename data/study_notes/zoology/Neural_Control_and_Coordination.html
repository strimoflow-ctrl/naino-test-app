🧠 1. Human Neural System

The human neural system is divided into two parts: Central Neural System (CNS) and Peripheral Neural System (PNS).

  • CNS: Includes the brain and the spinal cord. It is the site of information processing and control.
  • PNS: Comprises of all the nerves of the body associated with the CNS. The nerve fibres are of two types: Afferent (sensory) and Efferent (motor).

The PNS is further divided into Somatic neural system (relays impulses from CNS to skeletal muscles) and Autonomic neural system (relays impulses from CNS to involuntary organs and smooth muscles).

Neuron as Structural and Functional Unit

A neuron consists of a cell body, dendrites, and an axon. The cell body contains cytoplasm with typical cell organelles and certain granular bodies called Nissl's granules. The axon is a long fibre which transmits nerve impulses away from the cell body to a synapse or neuromuscular junction.

🚨 Exam Facts: Types of Neurons

Multipolar: One axon and two or more dendrites; found in the cerebral cortex.
Bipolar: One axon and one dendrite; found in the retina of eye.
Unipolar: Cell body with one axon only; found usually in the embryonic stage.

⚡ 2. Generation and Conduction of Nerve Impulse

Neurons are excitable cells because their membranes are in a polarized state. Ion channels on the membrane are selectively permeable.

Resting State

When a neuron is not conducting any impulse, the axonal membrane is comparatively more permeable to K⁺ ions and nearly impermeable to Na⁺ ions. Thus, the fluid outside has high Na⁺, and fluid inside has high K⁺ and negatively charged proteins. This maintains a resting membrane potential (inside is negative relative to outside).

This gradient is maintained by the active transport of ions by the Sodium-Potassium Pump which transports 3 Na⁺ outwards for 2 K⁺ into the cell.

Action Potential

When a stimulus is applied at a site, the membrane becomes freely permeable to Na⁺. This leads to a rapid influx of Na⁺, causing depolarisation. The electrical potential difference across the plasma membrane at this site is called the action potential or nerve impulse. Shortly after, permeability to Na⁺ falls and K⁺ rises, causing K⁺ to diffuse out and restore the resting potential (repolarisation).

🧠 3. Central Neural System (Brain)

The brain is the central information processing organ of our body. It is protected by the skull and cranial meninges: an outer dura mater, a very thin middle arachnoid, and an inner pia mater.

1. Forebrain

Consists of cerebrum, thalamus and hypothalamus.

  • Cerebrum: Forms the major part of the human brain. Divided into two cerebral hemispheres connected by a tract of nerve fibres called corpus callosum. The cerebral cortex contains motor areas, sensory areas and large regions called association areas (responsible for complex functions like memory and communication).
  • Thalamus: Major coordinating centre for sensory and motor signaling.
  • Hypothalamus: Controls body temperature, urge for eating and drinking. Also contains neurosecretory cells which secrete hypothalamic hormones.
  • Limbic System: Inner parts of cerebral hemispheres and a group of associated deep structures like amygdala, hippocampus, etc., form the limbic system. Involved in regulation of sexual behaviour, expression of emotional reactions (excitement, pleasure, rage and fear), and motivation.

2. Midbrain

Located between the thalamus/hypothalamus of the forebrain and pons of the hindbrain. A canal called the cerebral aqueduct passes through it. The dorsal portion consists mainly of four round swellings (lobes) called corpora quadrigemina.

3. Hindbrain

Consists of pons, cerebellum and medulla.

  • Pons: Interconnects different regions of the brain.
  • Cerebellum: Has very convoluted surface in order to provide the additional space for many more neurons. Coordinates voluntary movements and balance.
  • Medulla: Contains centres which control respiration, cardiovascular reflexes and gastric secretions.

👁️ 4. Sensory Reception and Processing (Eye)

The adult human eye ball is nearly a spherical structure. The wall of the eye ball is composed of three layers.

  • Sclera: The external layer composed of dense connective tissue. The anterior portion is called the cornea.
  • Choroid: Middle layer, contains many blood vessels and looks bluish. It is thick in the anterior part to form the ciliary body, which itself continues forward to form a pigmented and opaque structure called the iris. The eyeball contains a transparent crystalline lens.
  • Retina: Inner layer containing three layers of neural cells - ganglion cells, bipolar cells and photoreceptor cells (rods and cones).
    • Rods: Contain a purplish-red protein called rhodopsin (requires Vitamin A). Responsible for twilight (scotopic) vision.
    • Cones: Contain photopigments responding to red, green and blue lights. Responsible for daylight (photopic) vision and colour vision.
🤯 Amazing Info: Blind Spot and Fovea

The region where the optic nerve leaves the eye lacks photoreceptor cells and is called the Blind spot. But just slightly lateral to it is a yellowish pigmented spot called Macula lutea with a central pit called the Fovea, where visual acuity (resolution) is the greatest!

👂 5. The Ear (Hearing & Balance)

The ear performs two sensory functions: hearing and maintenance of body balance. Divided into three major sections: outer ear, middle ear and inner ear.

  • Outer Ear: Pinna and external auditory meatus (canal) which leads to the tympanic membrane (ear drum).
  • Middle Ear: Contains three ossicles called malleus, incus and stapes. The stapes is attached to the oval window of the cochlea. The Eustachian tube connects the middle ear cavity with the pharynx to equalize pressures.
  • Inner Ear: Fluid-filled inner ear called labyrinth consists of two parts: bony and membranous labyrinths. The coiled portion is called cochlea (hearing part). Inside cochlea is the Organ of Corti containing hair cells that act as auditory receptors.
  • Vestibular Apparatus: Located above the cochlea. Made of three semi-circular canals and the otolith organ. Responsible for maintaining body balance and posture!
💡 Short Trick: Ear Ossicles

Remember M-I-S (Malleus, Incus, Stapes). They amplify sound waves to the inner ear just like a MISsile!
