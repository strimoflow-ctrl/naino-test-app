🗑️ 1. Modes of Excretion

Animals accumulate ammonia, urea, uric acid, carbon dioxide, water, and ions like Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, phosphate, sulphate, etc. Ammonia, urea, and uric acid are the major forms of nitrogenous wastes.

  • Ammonotelic: Excrete ammonia. Most toxic, requires large amount of water for elimination. Generally excreted by diffusion across body surfaces or gill surfaces as ammonium ions. Examples: Many bony fishes, aquatic amphibians, and aquatic insects.
  • Ureotelic: Excrete urea. Ammonia produced by metabolism is converted into urea in the liver and released into blood to be excreted by kidneys. Examples: Mammals, many terrestrial amphibians, and marine fishes.
  • Uricotelic: Excrete uric acid in the form of pellet or paste with a minimum loss of water. Least toxic. Examples: Reptiles, birds, land snails, and insects.
🤯 Amazing Info: Shark Kidneys

Did you know? Sharks and other marine cartilaginous fishes actually retain a large amount of urea in their blood to maintain an osmotic balance with the salty ocean water! This prevents them from drying out.

🧑‍⚕️ 2. Human Excretory System

Consists of a pair of kidneys, one pair of ureters, a urinary bladder, and a urethra.

Kidneys

Reddish brown, bean-shaped structures situated between the levels of last thoracic and third lumbar vertebra. Each kidney has nearly one million complex tubular structures called nephrons (the functional units).

Internally, kidney is divided into an outer cortex and an inner medulla. The medulla is divided into a few conical masses (medullary pyramids) projecting into the calyces. The cortex extends in between the medullary pyramids as renal columns called Columns of Bertini.

The Nephron

Each nephron has two parts: the glomerulus and the renal tubule.

  • Glomerulus: A tuft of capillaries formed by the afferent arteriole. Blood from the glomerulus is carried away by an efferent arteriole.
  • Bowman's Capsule: A double-walled cup-like structure that encloses the glomerulus. Glomerulus along with Bowman's capsule is called the Malpighian body or renal corpuscle.
  • Renal Tubule: Consists of Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT), Henle's loop (descending and ascending limbs), and Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT). The DCTs of many nephrons open into a straight tube called the collecting duct.

💧 3. Urine Formation

Involves three main processes:

  1. Glomerular Filtration: First step. Occurs through 3 layers (endothelium of capillaries, epithelium of Bowman's capsule, and basement membrane). Epithelial cells (podocytes) leave minute spaces called filtration slits. It is an ultrafiltration. GFR in a healthy individual is approx. 125 ml/minute (180 litres/day).
  2. Reabsorption: Out of 180 L of filtrate formed per day, only 1.5 L of urine is released. This means nearly 99% of filtrate is reabsorbed by the renal tubules. PCT is lined by simple cuboidal brush border epithelium which increases surface area. Nearly all essential nutrients, and 70-80% of electrolytes and water are reabsorbed in PCT.
  3. Secretion: Tubular cells secrete substances like H⁺, K⁺ and ammonia into the filtrate to maintain ionic and acid-base balance of body fluids.
🚨 Exam Facts: Loop of Henle

Descending limb: Permeable to water but almost impermeable to electrolytes. Concentrates the filtrate as it moves down.
Ascending limb: Impermeable to water but allows transport of electrolytes. Dilutes the filtrate as it moves up.

⚖️ 4. Regulation of Kidney Function

  • Hypothalamus (ADH): Osmoreceptors in the body are activated by changes in blood volume. An excessive loss of fluid stimulates the hypothalamus to release ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone) or Vasopressin from the neurohypophysis. ADH facilitates water reabsorption from DCT and collecting duct, preventing diuresis.
  • JGA (Renin-Angiotensin mechanism): A fall in glomerular blood pressure activates the JG cells to release Renin. Renin converts angiotensinogen in blood to angiotensin I and further to angiotensin II. Angiotensin II is a powerful vasoconstrictor and also activates the adrenal cortex to release Aldosterone (causes reabsorption of Na⁺ and water from distal parts).
  • Heart (ANF): An increase in blood flow to the atria of the heart can cause the release of Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF). ANF causes vasodilation and thereby decreases blood pressure. It acts as a check on the renin-angiotensin mechanism.
💡 Short Trick: RAAS System

Renin converts Angiotensinogen to Angiotensin, which stimulates Secretion of Aldosterone! (RAAS)

🚑 5. Disorders of Excretory System

  • Uremia: Accumulation of urea in blood due to malfunctioning of kidneys. Highly harmful and may lead to kidney failure. Treated by Hemodialysis.
  • Renal Calculi: Stone or insoluble mass of crystallised salts (e.g., oxalates) formed within the kidney.
  • Glomerulonephritis: Inflammation of glomeruli of kidney.

In uremia patients, blood drained from a convenient artery is pumped into a dialysing unit containing a coiled cellophane tube suspended in a dialysing fluid. The porous cellophane membrane allows passage of molecules based on concentration gradient.
