🌍 1. Origin of Life

Evolutionary Biology is the study of history of life forms on earth. The Big Bang theory attempts to explain the origin of universe (a singular huge explosion). Earth was formed about 4.5 billion years back.

Oparin-Haldane Theory (Chemical Evolution)

They proposed that the first form of life could have come from pre-existing non-living organic molecules (e.g., RNA, protein, etc.) and that formation of life was preceded by chemical evolution (formation of diverse organic molecules from inorganic constituents). Conditions on earth were: High temperature, volcanic storms, reducing atmosphere containing CH₄, NH₃, etc.

Miller-Urey Experiment (1953)

S.L. Miller created similar conditions in a laboratory scale. He created electric discharge in a closed flask containing CH₄, H₂, NH₃ and water vapour at 800°C. He observed the formation of amino acids.

🤯 Amazing Info: Theory of Panspermia

Did you know? Early Greek thinkers thought units of life called spores were transferred to different planets including earth. This 'Panspermia' theory is still a favourite idea for some astronomers!

🦴 2. Evidences for Evolution

Homologous Organs

Organs which have the same fundamental structure but different functions. Indicates common ancestry. They are a result of Divergent Evolution.

  • Forelimbs of Whale (swimming), Bat (flying), Cheetah (running) and Man (grasping). All have similar bones: humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals and phalanges.
  • Vertebrate hearts or brains.
  • In plants: Thorns of Bougainvillea and tendrils of Cucurbita.
Fig: Homologous Organs (Divergent Evolution)

Analogous Organs

Organs which have different anatomical structures but perform similar functions. They are a result of Convergent Evolution.

  • Wings of butterfly and of birds.
  • Eye of the octopus and of mammals.
  • Flippers of Penguins and Dolphins.
  • Sweet potato (root modification) and potato (stem modification).
💡 Short Trick: Evolution Types

H D  |  A C
Homologous = Divergent
Analogous = Convergent

🧬 3. Adaptive Radiation

The process of evolution of different species in a given geographical area starting from a point and literally radiating to other areas of geography (habitats) is called adaptive radiation.

  • Darwin's Finches: From original seed-eating features, many other forms with altered beaks arose, enabling them to become insectivorous and vegetarian finches. Found in Galapagos Islands.
  • Australian Marsupials: A number of marsupials, each different from the other evolved from an ancestral stock, but all within the Australian island continent.

Note: When more than one adaptive radiation appeared to have occurred in an isolated geographical area (representing different habitats), one can call this Convergent Evolution (e.g., Placental mammals and Australian marsupials).

🔬 4. Biological Evolution & Mechanism

Natural Selection (Darwin)

The essence of Darwinian theory about evolution is natural selection. Two key concepts of Darwinian Theory of Evolution are Branching descent and Natural selection. Microbes that divide fast have the ability to multiply and become millions of individuals within hours. A colony of bacteria (say A) growing on a given medium has built-in variation in terms of ability to utilise a feed component. A change in the medium composition would bring out only that part of the population (say B) that can survive under the new conditions. Thus fitness of B is better than A under the new conditions.

Mutation Theory (Hugo de Vries)

He worked on evening primrose and brought forth the idea of mutations. He believed that it is mutation which causes evolution and not the minor variations (heritable) that Darwin talked about.

  • Darwinian variations: Small and directional. Evolution is gradual.
  • De Vries mutations: Random and directionless. Evolution is sudden (Saltation - single step large mutation).

📈 5. Hardy-Weinberg Principle

This principle says that allele frequencies in a population are stable and is constant from generation to generation. The gene pool remains a constant. This is called genetic equilibrium.

Formula: p² + 2pq + q² = 1
(Where p and q represent the frequencies of allele A and allele a in a diploid).
p² = frequency of homozygous dominant (AA)
q² = frequency of homozygous recessive (aa)
2pq = frequency of heterozygous (Aa).

Factors Affecting Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

Five factors are known to affect Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium:

  1. Gene migration or gene flow
  2. Genetic drift: Change in allele frequency by chance. Sometimes the change is so different in the new sample of population that they become a different species. The original drifted population becomes founders and the effect is called founder effect.
  3. Mutation
  4. Genetic recombination
  5. Natural selection
