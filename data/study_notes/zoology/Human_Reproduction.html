🚹 1. Male Reproductive System

Located in the pelvis region. It includes a pair of testes along with accessory ducts, glands and the external genitalia.

Testes

Situated outside the abdominal cavity within a pouch called scrotum. Scrotum helps in maintaining the low temperature of the testes (2-2.5 °C lower than normal internal body temperature), necessary for spermatogenesis.

  • Each testis has about 250 compartments called testicular lobules.
  • Each lobule contains 1 to 3 highly coiled seminiferous tubules where sperms are produced.
  • Inner lining of seminiferous tubules has:
    1. Male germ cells (spermatogonia): Undergo meiotic divisions finally leading to sperm formation.
    2. Sertoli cells: Provide nutrition to the germ cells.
  • The regions outside the seminiferous tubules called interstitial spaces contain Leydig cells. Leydig cells synthesize and secrete testicular hormones called androgens (testosterone).

Accessory Ducts & Glands

Ducts: Rete testis -> Vasa efferentia -> Epididymis -> Vas deferens.
Glands: Seminal vesicles (paired), Prostate (single), Bulbourethral glands (paired). Secretions of these glands constitute the seminal plasma which is rich in fructose, calcium and certain enzymes.

💡 Short Trick: Pathway of Sperm

S-E-V-E-N U-P
Seminiferous tubules → Epididymis → Vas deferens → Ejaculatory duct → N(Nothing) → Urethra → Penis.

🚺 2. Female Reproductive System

Located in the pelvic region. Consists of a pair of ovaries, a pair of oviducts (fallopian tubes), uterus, cervix, vagina, and external genitalia. Supported by mammary glands.

Ovaries & Oviducts

  • Ovaries: Primary female sex organs that produce the female gamete (ovum) and ovarian hormones (estrogen, progesterone).
  • Fallopian Tubes (Oviducts): About 10-12 cm long. Parts:
    • Infundibulum: Funnel-shaped part closer to ovary. Edges possess finger-like projections called fimbriae (help in collection of ovum after ovulation).
    • Ampulla: Wider part. This is the site of fertilization!
    • Isthmus: Narrow part joining the uterus.

Uterus (Womb)

Single, shaped like an inverted pear. The wall of the uterus has three layers of tissue:

  1. Perimetrium: External thin membranous layer.
  2. Myometrium: Middle thick layer of smooth muscle. Exhibits strong contraction during delivery of the baby.
  3. Endometrium: Inner glandular layer. Undergoes cyclical changes during menstrual cycle. Implantation of blastocyst occurs here.

🧬 3. Gametogenesis

Spermatogenesis

Process of formation of sperms in seminiferous tubules. Begins at puberty due to significant increase in GnRH.
Spermatogonia (2n) -> Primary Spermatocytes (2n) -> Meiosis I -> Secondary Spermatocytes (n) -> Meiosis II -> Spermatids (n) -> Spermiogenesis -> Spermatozoa (n).

Oogenesis

Process of formation of a mature female gamete. It is initiated during the embryonic development stage when millions of oogonia are formed within each fetal ovary. No more oogonia are formed and added after birth.

Oogonia -> Primary Oocyte (arrested in Prophase I of Meiosis I). At puberty, out of many, only 60,000-80,000 primary follicles are left in each ovary. Every month, one completes Meiosis I resulting in a large Secondary Oocyte (n) and a tiny First Polar Body.

📅 4. Menstrual Cycle

The reproductive cycle in the female primates (e.g., monkeys, apes and human beings) is called menstrual cycle. Menarche is the first menstruation. Menopause is cessation of the cycle (~50 years of age).

  1. Menstrual Phase (Days 1-5): Breakdown of endometrial lining of uterus and its blood vessels. Occurs only if the released ovum is not fertilised.
  2. Follicular / Proliferative Phase (Days 6-13): Primary follicles grow to become fully mature Graafian follicle. Endometrium regenerates. LH and FSH increase gradually. Secretion of estrogens increases.
  3. Ovulatory Phase (Day 14): Rapid secretion of LH leading to its maximum level during the mid-cycle called LH surge. This induces rupture of Graafian follicle and release of ovum (ovulation).
  4. Luteal / Secretory Phase (Days 15-28): Remaining parts of Graafian follicle transform into Corpus Luteum. It secretes large amounts of Progesterone which is essential for maintenance of the endometrium for implantation.
🚨 Exam Facts: Hormone peaks

During the Menstrual Cycle:
- Estrogen peaks just BEFORE ovulation.
- LH and FSH peak ON the day of ovulation (Day 14).
- Progesterone peaks AFTER ovulation (during luteal phase).

👶 5. Fertilisation, Implantation & Pregnancy

Fertilisation

The fusion of a sperm with an ovum. Takes place in the ampullary region of the fallopian tube. The sperm induces changes in the zona pellucida layer of the ovum that block the entry of additional sperms (prevents polyspermy).

Implantation

The zygote undergoes mitotic division (cleavage) moving towards uterus to form 2, 4, 8, 16 daughter cells called blastomeres. Embryo with 8-16 blastomeres is a Morula. It transforms into a Blastocyst. The outer layer (trophoblast) attaches to the endometrium, and inner cell mass gets differentiated as the embryo. This attachment is called implantation.

Placenta & Hormones

Placenta is the structural and functional unit between developing embryo and maternal body. It produces several hormones like hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin), hPL (human placental lactogen), estrogens, progesterones. hCG, hPL and relaxin are produced in women ONLY during pregnancy.

🤯 Amazing Info: Colostrum

The milk produced during the initial few days of lactation is called colostrum. It contains several antibodies (especially IgA) absolutely essential to develop resistance for the new-born babies!
