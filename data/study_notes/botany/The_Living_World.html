🌱 1. What is Living?

Living phenomena are an expression of interacting chemical reactions. To identify living organisms, we study their fundamental properties.

Properties of Living Organisms

  • Growth: Increase in mass and increase in number of individuals (cells).
    Note: In plants, growth by cell division is continuous throughout life. In animals, growth is seen up to a certain age. However, non-living things also increase in mass (e.g., mountains, sand dunes).
  • Reproduction: Production of offspring similar to the parent.
    Note: Many non-living things can replicate (e.g., crystals), and many living organisms cannot reproduce (e.g., mules, sterile worker bees, infertile human couples). Therefore, reproduction is NOT a defining property.
  • Metabolism: Sum total of all chemical reactions occurring in our body.
    Note: No other non-living object is known to perform metabolism. Metabolism is a DEFINING property. (Even in a test tube, isolated metabolic reactions are living reactions, but not living things).
  • Cellular Organization: Body is made of cells. (Found in almost all organisms, from prokaryotes to eukaryotes).
  • Consciousness: Ability to sense environment and respond to stimuli.
    Note: All organisms (from prokaryotes to eukaryotes) can sense light, temperature, water, chemicals. Humans are unique because they are self-conscious. Consciousness is a DEFINING property.
🚨 NEET HIGH-YIELD: Defining Properties

Metabolism and Consciousness are the only two DEFINING properties of living organisms. Growth and reproduction cannot be defining properties because they occur in non-living things or are absent in some living things.

🌍 2. Biodiversity & Nomenclature

Diversity in the Living World

  • Number of species known and described varies between 1.7 to 1.8 million.
  • This number refers to Biodiversity (Biological diversity).
  • Every local region has its own flora and fauna.
  • IUCN Red Data Book: Keeps a record of extinct and endangered animals and plants.

Nomenclature

Local names vary from region to region, causing confusion. Therefore, we need standardized, universally accepted scientific names.

Binomial Nomenclature

Proposed by Carolus Linnaeus. Every organism has a two-word name:

  1. Generic name (Genus): Starts with a capital letter.
  2. Specific epithet (Species): Starts with a small letter.

Rules: Names are derived from Latin/Greek, written in Italics (or underlined separately if handwritten).

📖 Key Organizations
  • ICBN: International Code of Botanical Nomenclature (for plants).
  • ICZN: International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (for animals).

🚨 NEET TRAP: Suffixes in Taxonomy

Questions are frequently asked on the suffixes used for different categories, especially in Plants:

  • Family: Ends in -aceae (e.g., Solanaceae, Poaceae). Exceptions: Leguminosae, Compositae, Palmae, Cruciferae.
  • Order: Ends in -ales (e.g., Polymoniales).
  • Class: Ends in -opsida (e.g., Algae), -phyceae, -ae (Animals).

🔬 4. Taxonomical Aids

Tools and techniques used to study, identify, and classify organisms.

1. Herbarium

  • A storehouse of collected plant specimens that are dried, pressed, and preserved on sheets.
  • Sheets are arranged according to a universally accepted system of classification.
  • Contains a label with: Date & place of collection, English/local/botanical names, family, collector's name.
  • Serves as a quick referral system for taxonomic studies.

2. Botanical Gardens

  • Have collections of living plants for reference.
  • Plants are grown for identification purposes.
📍 Famous Botanical Gardens (Must Memorize)
  • Indian Botanical Garden: Howrah (West Bengal).
  • National Botanical Research Institute (NBRI): Lucknow.
  • Royal Botanic Garden: Kew (England).
  • Royal Botanic Garden: Edinburgh (Scotland).

3. Museum

  • Has collections of preserved plant and animal specimens.
  • Specimens are preserved in jars with preservative solutions or on pins/mounts.
  • Insects are preserved in insect boxes after killing, pinning, and drying.
  • Often have stuffed birds and animals and large skeletons.

4. Zoological Park (Zoos)

  • Have collections of live wild animals.
  • Animals are kept in conditions similar to their natural habitats.
📍 Famous Zoological Park
  • National Zoological Park: New Delhi.

5. Key (Taxonomic Key)

  • Analytical tool used for identification of organisms based on similarities and dissimilarities.
  • Based on contrasting characters generally in a pair called a couplet.
  • Each statement in the key is called a lead.
  • Separate taxonomic keys are required for each taxonomic category (Family, Genus, Species).

Other Aids (Briefly)

  • Flora: Actual account of habitat and distribution of plants of a given area. Provides index to plant species.
  • Manuals: Provide information for the identification of names of species found in an area.
  • Monographs: Provide information on any one taxon (e.g., a specific family or genus).

  • Nomenclature ICBN for plants, ICZN for animals. Binomial nomenclature by Carolus Linnaeus.
  • Suffixes Plant Family ends in -aceae. Order ends in -ales.
  • Herbarium Dried, pressed, preserved plant specimens on sheets.
  • Botanical Garden Living plants (e.g., Howrah, Kew, Lucknow).
  • Museum Preserved dead specimens (jars, pins, stuffed animals).
  • Zoo Live wild animals (e.g., National Zoological Park, Delhi).
  • Key Uses couplets and leads for identification.
