🔬 1. Introduction to Respiration

Cellular respiration is the mechanism of breakdown of food materials within the cell to release energy, and the trapping of this energy for synthesis of ATP. Plants do not have specialized organs for gaseous exchange but have stomata and lenticels.

Complete combustion of glucose produces CO₂ and H₂O as end products and yields energy, most of which is given out as heat. But the cell breaks down glucose in multiple steps to trap this energy as ATP.

🔄 2. Glycolysis (EMP Pathway)

The term glycolysis has originated from the Greek words, glycos for sugar, and lysis for splitting. Given by Gustav Embden, Otto Meyerhof, and J. Parnas.

  • Occurs in the cytoplasm of the cell and is present in all living organisms.
  • In this process, glucose undergoes partial oxidation to form two molecules of pyruvic acid.
  • In plants, glucose is derived from sucrose (end product of photosynthesis). Sucrose is converted into glucose and fructose by the enzyme invertase.
Fig: Fate of Pyruvic Acid based on Oxygen availability

Energy Yield of Glycolysis

  • Net gain of ATP directly synthesized = 2 ATP.
  • Net production of NADH = 2 NADH.
  • Key regulatory enzyme: Phosphofructokinase (PFK) (Pacemaker enzyme of glycolysis).

🍾 3. Fermentation (Anaerobic)

Incomplete oxidation of glucose under anaerobic conditions.

  • Alcoholic Fermentation: Occurs in yeast. Pyruvic acid is converted to ethanol and CO₂. Enzymes: Pyruvic acid decarboxylase and alcohol dehydrogenase.
  • Lactic Acid Fermentation: Occurs in some bacteria and muscle cells under anaerobic conditions. Pyruvic acid is reduced to lactic acid by lactate dehydrogenase.

In both types, not much energy is released (less than 7% of energy in glucose is released and not all of it is trapped as high energy bonds of ATP).

🤯 Amazing Info: Yeast Poisoning

Did you know? Yeasts poison themselves to death when the concentration of alcohol reaches about 13 percent. This is why naturally fermented beverages cannot have an alcohol content higher than this without distillation!

🌬️ 4. Aerobic Respiration

Takes place within the mitochondria. Final product of glycolysis (pyruvate) is transported from cytoplasm into the mitochondria.

Link Reaction

Pyruvate undergoes oxidative decarboxylation catalyzed by pyruvate dehydrogenase. It produces Acetyl CoA, NADH, and CO₂.

Krebs Cycle (TCA Cycle)

Discovered by Hans Krebs. Occurs in mitochondrial matrix.

  • First stable product: Citric Acid (6C).
  • The cycle involves condensation of Acetyl CoA (2C) with Oxaloacetic Acid (OAA - 4C) and water to yield citric acid.
  • At 3 points in the cycle, NAD⁺ is reduced to NADH. At 1 point, FAD⁺ is reduced to FADH₂.
  • One molecule of GTP (ATP) is synthesized directly via substrate-level phosphorylation when succinyl-CoA is converted to succinic acid.

⚡ 5. Electron Transport System (ETS)

The metabolic pathway through which the electron passes from one carrier to another, located on the inner mitochondrial membrane.

  • Complex I: NADH dehydrogenase.
  • Complex II: Succinate dehydrogenase.
  • Complex III: Cytochrome bc₁ complex.
  • Complex IV: Cytochrome c oxidase complex (containing cytochromes a and a₃, and two copper centers).
  • Complex V: ATP synthase.

Role of Oxygen: O₂ acts as the final hydrogen acceptor. The whole process of oxygen driving the synthesis of ATP is called Oxidative Phosphorylation.

🚨 Exam Facts: ATP Yield

Oxidation of 1 molecule of NADH gives rise to 3 molecules of ATP.
Oxidation of 1 molecule of FADH₂ produces 2 molecules of ATP.
Total net gain of ATP from 1 glucose molecule during aerobic respiration is roughly 38 ATP (or 36 ATP depending on the shuttle system).

Amphibolic Pathway

Respiratory pathway is involved in both anabolism (synthesis) and catabolism (breakdown), hence it is better considered an amphibolic pathway rather than purely catabolic.
