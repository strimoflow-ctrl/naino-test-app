🧬 1. DNA: The Genetic Material

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is the genetic material in most of the organisms. RNA mostly functions as a messenger, though it acts as genetic material in some viruses (e.g., TMV, HIV, QB bacteriophage).

Structure of Polynucleotide Chain

  • A nucleotide has three components: A nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group.
  • Nitrogenous base is linked to the OH of 1'C pentose sugar through an N-glycosidic linkage to form a nucleoside.
  • Phosphate group is linked to OH of 5'C of a nucleoside through phosphoester linkage to form a nucleotide.
  • Two nucleotides are linked through 3'-5' phosphodiester linkage.

Double Helix Model

Proposed by Watson and Crick (1953) based on X-ray diffraction data produced by Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin.

  • Made of two polynucleotide chains where the backbone is sugar-phosphate, and bases project inside.
  • The two chains have anti-parallel polarity (one is 5'->3', other is 3'->5').
  • Bases are paired through Hydrogen bonds: A pairs with T (2 H-bonds); G pairs with C (3 H-bonds).
  • Pitch of the helix is 3.4 nm. There are roughly 10 bp in each turn. The distance between a bp in a helix is approximately 0.34 nm.
🚨 Exam Facts: Chargaff's Rule

Erwin Chargaff observed that for a double stranded DNA, the ratios between Adenine and Thymine and Guanine and Cytosine are constant and equals one. [A + G] = [T + C] or Purines = Pyrimidines.

🔍 2. The Search for Genetic Material

Griffith's Transforming Principle (1928)

Frederick Griffith experimented with Streptococcus pneumoniae. He used two strains: Smooth (S) strain (virulent, has capsule) and Rough (R) strain (non-virulent).

  • S strain -> Mice die.
  • R strain -> Mice live.
  • Heat-killed S strain -> Mice live.
  • Heat-killed S + live R strain -> Mice die! He recovered live S bacteria from dead mice.

He concluded that the R strain had been 'transformed' by a transforming principle from the heat-killed S strain.

Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty (1944)

Purified biochemicals (proteins, DNA, RNA) from heat-killed S cells. Discovered that proteases and RNases did not affect transformation, but DNase inhibited transformation. Hence, DNA is the hereditary material.

Hershey-Chase Experiment (1952)

Unequivocal proof that DNA is the genetic material came from experiments on bacteriophages. They used radioactive Phosphorus (³²P) to label DNA and radioactive Sulfur (³⁵S) to label protein coats. Only ³²P was found inside the infected bacteria, proving DNA entered the cell.

⚙️ 3. Central Dogma of Molecular Biology

Proposed by Francis Crick. It states that genetic information flows from DNA to RNA to Protein.

Fig: The Central Dogma (Proposed by Francis Crick)
🤯 Amazing Info: Reverse Transcription

Did you know? Certain viruses (like HIV, a retrovirus) break this rule! They have RNA as genetic material and use an enzyme called Reverse Transcriptase to synthesize DNA from RNA. This is called the Reverse Central Dogma.

🛠️ 4. Processes: Replication, Transcription & Translation

Replication

Occurs in the S-phase of cell cycle. It is Semi-conservative (proved by Meselson and Stahl using ¹⁵N heavy isotope of nitrogen in E.coli).
Main enzyme is DNA-dependent DNA polymerase. It polymerizes only in 5'->3' direction. Therefore, on one strand (3'->5' template) replication is continuous (Leading strand), while on the other (5'->3' template) it is discontinuous (Lagging strand), forming Okazaki fragments which are joined by DNA ligase.

Transcription

The process of copying genetic info from one strand of DNA into RNA. Enzyme used is DNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
A transcription unit has three regions in DNA: Promoter, Structural gene, and Terminator. Only the template strand (3'->5') is transcribed. The coding strand (5'->3') has the same sequence as RNA (except T instead of U).

  • In eukaryotes, there are 3 RNA polymerases (I transcribes rRNA, II transcribes mRNA precursor hnRNA, III transcribes tRNA).
  • Post-transcriptional modifications: Splicing (introns removed, exons joined), Capping (methyl guanosine triphosphate added to 5' end), Tailing (poly-A tail added at 3' end).

Translation

Process of polymerisation of amino acids to form a polypeptide. The order and sequence of amino acids are defined by the sequence of bases in the mRNA.
Requires Ribosomes (the protein factory) and tRNA (the adapter molecule that reads the codon and brings the amino acid).

💡 Short Trick: Stop Codons

UAA, UAG, UGA are Stop/Nonsense Codons.
Remember: U Are Away, U Are Gone, U Go Away!

🎛️ 5. Regulation of Gene Expression (Lac Operon)

Elucidated by Francois Jacob and Jacque Monod. An operon is a polycistronic structural gene regulated by a common promoter and regulatory genes.

  • The Lac Operon: Consists of one regulatory gene (i gene - codes for repressor) and three structural genes (z, y, a).
  • z gene: codes for beta-galactosidase (breaks down lactose into galactose and glucose).
  • y gene: codes for permease (increases permeability of cell to beta-galactosides).
  • a gene: codes for a transacetylase.

Mechanism: In absence of lactose, the repressor binds to the operator region and prevents RNA polymerase from transcribing the operon. When lactose (inducer) is present, it binds to the repressor and inactivates it, allowing transcription to proceed. This is called negative regulation.
