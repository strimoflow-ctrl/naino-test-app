🍞 1. Carbohydrates

Optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones. General formula: Cx(H2O)y.

Classification:

  • Monosaccharides: Simplest, cannot be hydrolyzed. E.g., Glucose (Aldohexose), Fructose (Ketohexose).
  • Disaccharides: Yield two monosaccharides on hydrolysis. Linked by Glycosidic Linkage (an oxide linkage).
    • Sucrose → α-D-Glucose + β-D-Fructose (Non-reducing sugar).
    • Maltose → α-D-Glucose + α-D-Glucose (Reducing sugar).
    • Lactose → β-D-Galactose + β-D-Glucose (Reducing sugar).
  • Polysaccharides: Polymers of monosaccharides. E.g., Starch (Amylose + Amylopectin), Cellulose, Glycogen.
🚨 Exam Facts: Glucose Reactions
  • With HI/Heat → n-Hexane (Proves 6 carbons in a straight chain).
  • With Bromine water → Gluconic acid (Proves presence of aldehyde group).
  • With HNO3 (Nitric acid) → Saccharic acid (Oxidizes both -CHO and terminal -CH2OH).

🥩 2. Proteins

Polymers of α-amino acids linked by Peptide Bonds (-CO-NH-).

  • Essential Amino Acids: Cannot be synthesized in the body, must be taken in diet. (e.g., Valine, Leucine).
  • Zwitterion: In aqueous solution, carboxyl group loses a proton and amino group accepts it, forming a dipolar ion. It is neutral but contains both + and - charges.

Structure of Proteins

  • Primary: Sequence of amino acids.
  • Secondary: Local folding (e.g., α-helix and β-pleated sheet) stabilized by Hydrogen bonds.
  • Tertiary: Overall 3D shape (folding of secondary structure). Stabilized by H-bonds, disulfide linkages, van der Waals, and electrostatic forces.
  • Denaturation: Loss of biological activity due to unfolding of 2° and 3° structure (1° structure remains intact) caused by heat, pH change. (e.g., Coagulation of egg white).

🧬 3. Nucleic Acids (DNA & RNA)

Polymers of Nucleotides. A Nucleotide = Pentose Sugar + Nitrogenous Base + Phosphate group.

(A Nucleoside = Base + Sugar only).

  • DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid): Sugar is β-D-2-deoxyribose. Bases: Adenine (A), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C), Thymine (T). Double helix structure.
  • RNA (Ribonucleic Acid): Sugar is β-D-ribose. Bases: Adenine (A), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C), Uracil (U). Usually single stranded.

Phosphodiester Linkage: Joins two nucleotides between 5' and 3' carbon atoms of the pentose sugar.

💊 4. Vitamins

Organic compounds required in small amounts for normal growth and health.

  • Fat-soluble: Vitamins A, D, E, K. Stored in liver and adipose tissues.
  • Water-soluble: B group vitamins and Vitamin C. Must be supplied regularly as they are excreted in urine (Except Vitamin B12).
💡 Deficiency Diseases
  • Vit A: Night blindness, Xerophthalmia.
  • Vit B1 (Thiamine): Beri-beri.
  • Vit C (Ascorbic acid): Scurvy.
  • Vit D: Rickets (children), Osteomalacia (adults).
  • Vit B12: Pernicious anaemia.
