Redox Reactions

Class 11 Chemistry | Chapter 7

🔄 1. Oxidation and Reduction

Redox reactions are those in which oxidation and reduction take place simultaneously.

💡 Trick: LEO the lion says GER

LEO: Loss of Electrons is Oxidation.
GER: Gain of Electrons is Reduction.

Alternatively: OIL RIG (Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain).

  • Oxidizing Agent (Oxidant): The substance that gains electrons and gets reduced.
  • Reducing Agent (Reductant): The substance that loses electrons and gets oxidized.

🔢 2. Oxidation Number (State)

The apparent charge an atom possesses in a molecule or ion based on electronegativity differences.

Rules for Assigning Oxidation Number:

  1. Elements in free state (e.g., H2, O2, Na) have ON = 0.
  2. Fluorine (F) is always -1 in its compounds.
  3. Alkali metals (Group 1) are always +1. Alkaline earth metals (Group 2) are always +2.
  4. Oxygen is usually -2. Exceptions: Peroxides (O22-) it is -1; Superoxides (O2-) it is -1/2; with Fluorine (OF2) it is +2.
  5. Hydrogen is usually +1. Exception: In metal hydrides (NaH), it is -1.
  6. The algebraic sum of ONs of all atoms in a molecule is 0, and in a polyatomic ion is equal to the net charge on the ion.
🚨 Exam Facts: Paradoxical Fractional ON

Sometimes ON comes out fractional (e.g., Br3O8 where Br is +16/3). This is an average oxidation state. In reality, different atoms of the same element in the molecule have different integer oxidation states based on their structure.

⚖️ 3. Types of Redox Reactions

  • Combination: A + B → C (e.g., C + O2 → CO2)
  • Decomposition: C → A + B (e.g., 2KClO3 → 2KCl + 3O2)
  • Displacement: X + YZ → XZ + Y. Can be metal displacement (Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu) or non-metal displacement.
  • Disproportionation: A single substance is simultaneously oxidized and reduced. (e.g., 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2). Oxygen goes from -1 to -2 and 0.

🔋 4. Electrochemical Cells (Introduction)

A device that converts chemical energy of a spontaneous redox reaction into electrical energy (Galvanic/Voltaic cell) or vice-versa (Electrolytic cell).

Daniel Cell (Zn-Cu Cell):

  • Anode: Zinc rod. Oxidation takes place. (Zn → Zn2+ + 2e-). Negative terminal.
  • Cathode: Copper rod. Reduction takes place. (Cu2+ + 2e- → Cu). Positive terminal.
  • Salt Bridge: A U-tube containing inert electrolyte (KNO3, KCl in agar-agar). It completes the circuit and maintains electrical neutrality in both half-cells without mixing the solutions.
💡 Trick: L-O-A-N

In a Galvanic Cell: Left side → Oxidation → Anode → Negative.
