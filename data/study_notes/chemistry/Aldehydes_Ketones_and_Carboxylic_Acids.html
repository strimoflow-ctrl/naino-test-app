Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids

Class 12 Chemistry | Chapter 8

⚗️ 1. Important Name Reactions (Preparation)

  • Rosenmund Reduction: Acyl chloride (R-COCl) is hydrogenated over catalyst, Pd on BaSO4 to give Aldehyde.
  • Stephen Reaction: Nitriles (R-CN) reduced to corresponding imines with SnCl2/HCl, which on hydrolysis give Aldehydes.
  • Etard Reaction: Chromyl chloride (CrO2Cl2) oxidizes methyl group of Toluene to form Benzaldehyde.
  • Gatterman-Koch Reaction: Benzene + CO + HCl in presence of Anhy. AlCl3/CuCl gives Benzaldehyde.
  • Clemmensen Reduction: Carbonyl group is reduced to CH2 group (Alkane) using Zn-Hg and conc. HCl.
  • Wolff-Kishner Reduction: Carbonyl group is reduced to CH2 group using Hydrazine (NH2NH2) followed by KOH/Ethylene glycol.

🧪 2. Key Chemical Properties

Nucleophilic Addition

The >C=O bond is polar. Nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbon. Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones due to less steric hindrance and less +I effect.

Oxidation (Tollens' and Fehling's Test)

Used to distinguish aldehydes from ketones. Aldehydes are easily oxidized, ketones are not.

  • Tollens' Test: Ammoniacal silver nitrate [Ag(NH3)2]+. Aldehydes reduce it to metallic silver forming a Silver Mirror.
  • Fehling's Test: Fehling A (CuSO4) + Fehling B (Rochelle salt). Aldehydes reduce Cu2+ to Cu+ forming a Reddish-Brown ppt of Cu2O. (Note: Aromatic aldehydes like benzaldehyde do NOT respond to Fehling's test).
🚨 Exam Facts: Iodoform Test

Given by compounds containing CH3-CO- group (methyl ketones) or CH3-CH(OH)- group. They react with I2/NaOH (or NaOI) to give a yellow precipitate of Iodoform (CHI3).

💥 3. Reactions due to α-Hydrogen

The α-hydrogen in aldehydes/ketones is acidic due to electron withdrawing effect of carbonyl group and resonance stabilization of the conjugate base (enolate ion).

  • Aldol Condensation: Aldehydes/Ketones having at least ONE α-hydrogen react in presence of dilute alkali (dil. NaOH) to form β-hydroxy aldehyde (aldol) or β-hydroxy ketone (ketol). On heating, they lose water to form α,β-unsaturated compounds.
  • Cannizzaro Reaction: Aldehydes having NO α-hydrogen (e.g., Formaldehyde HCHO, Benzaldehyde C6H5CHO) react with conc. alkali (conc. NaOH). It is a disproportionation reaction yielding an alcohol and a salt of a carboxylic acid.

🍋 4. Carboxylic Acids (R-COOH)

Acidity

Carboxylic acids are more acidic than phenols because the carboxylate ion is stabilized by two equivalent resonance structures (negative charge is on two electronegative oxygen atoms).

Effect of Substituents: Electron Withdrawing Groups (EWG like -NO2, -Cl) increase acidity. Electron Donating Groups (EDG like -CH3) decrease acidity.

Important Reactions

  • Esterification: Acid + Alcohol (in presence of conc. H2SO4) ↔ Ester + Water.
  • Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction: Carboxylic acids having an α-hydrogen are halogenated at the α-position on treatment with Cl2/Br2 in the presence of small amount of Red Phosphorus. Forms α-halo carboxylic acids.
