d and f Block Elements

🛠️ 1. Transition Elements (d-block)

Elements which have incompletely filled d-orbitals in their ground state or in any of their oxidation states.

🚨 Exam Facts: Zinc, Cadmium, Mercury

Zn, Cd, and Hg have completely filled d-orbitals (d10) in ground state as well as in their common oxidation states. Hence, they are NOT considered as transition elements.

General Characteristics:

  • Variable Oxidation States: Due to small energy difference between (n-1)d and ns orbitals. Mn shows max oxidation states (+2 to +7).
  • Catalytic Properties: Due to their ability to adopt multiple oxidation states and provide surface area (e.g., V2O5 in Contact Process, finely divided Fe in Haber's Process).
  • Formation of Colored Ions: Due to presence of unpaired electrons, causing d-d transition of electrons absorbing light in the visible region. (Sc3+, Ti4+, Zn2+ are colorless as they have d0 or d10 config).
  • Magnetic Properties: Mostly paramagnetic due to unpaired electrons. Magnetic moment μ = √[n(n+2)] BM (Bohr Magnetons), where n is number of unpaired electrons.
  • Formation of Interstitial Compounds: Small atoms like H, C, or N are trapped inside the crystal lattices of metals. They are hard, have high melting points, and retain metallic conductivity.

🌡️ 2. Potassium Dichromate (K2Cr2O7)

A powerful oxidizing agent in acidic medium.

Reaction in acidic medium:
Cr2O72- + 14H+ + 6e- → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O

Color change: Orange (Cr6+) to Green (Cr3+).

pH Dependence

Chromate (yellow) and Dichromate (orange) are interconvertible depending on pH.

2CrO42- (Yellow) + 2H+ ↔ Cr2O72- (Orange) + H2O

In acidic medium, orange dichromate exists. In basic medium, yellow chromate exists.

🟣 3. Potassium Permanganate (KMnO4)

Intense purple/dark violet solid. Very strong oxidizing agent.

In Acidic Medium (Strongest): MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- → Mn2+ + 4H2O (Colorless). Equivalent weight = M/5.

In Neutral/Faintly Alkaline Medium: MnO4- + 2H2O + 3e- → MnO2 (Brown ppt) + 4OH-. Equivalent weight = M/3.

In Strongly Alkaline Medium: MnO4- + e- → MnO42- (Green Manganate). Equivalent weight = M/1.

🌌 4. Inner Transition Elements (f-block)

Lanthanoids (4f series, Ce to Lu)

General oxidation state is +3. (+2 and +4 are also shown by some elements to achieve f0, f7, or f14 stability, e.g., Ce4+, Eu2+).

💡 Lanthanoid Contraction

The steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii from Lanthanum to Lutetium is called Lanthanoid Contraction. Reason: Poor shielding effect of 4f electrons.

Consequences: Similarity in radii of 4d and 5d series elements (e.g., Zr and Hf have almost same radii). Difficulty in separation of lanthanoids.

Actinoids (5f series, Th to Lr)

All are radioactive. They show a larger number of oxidation states than lanthanoids because 5f, 6d, and 7s levels are of comparable energies.
