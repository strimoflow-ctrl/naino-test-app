🛢️ 1. Alkanes (Paraffins)

Saturated open chain hydrocarbons containing carbon-carbon single bonds. General formula: CnH2n+2.

Key Preparations:

  • Wurtz Reaction: 2RX + 2Na (in dry ether) → R-R + 2NaX. (Used to prepare higher alkanes with EVEN number of carbon atoms. Methane cannot be prepared).
  • Decarboxylation: RCOONa + NaOH(CaO) → R-H + Na2CO3. (Alkane formed has one carbon less than the salt).
  • Kolbe's Electrolysis: Electrolysis of aqueous solution of sodium/potassium salt of carboxylic acid gives alkane at anode.

Chemical Properties:

Undergo Free Radical Substitution (e.g., Halogenation). Reactivity of halogens: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2.

💡 Conformations of Ethane

Infinite spatial arrangements possible due to free rotation around C-C single bond.

  • Staggered: Minimum repulsion, most stable.
  • Eclipsed: Maximum repulsion, least stable.

⚡ 2. Alkenes (Olefins)

Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one double bond. General formula: CnH2n.

Key Preparations:

  • Dehydrohalogenation (from Alkyl Halides): Reaction with alcoholic KOH gives alkene (β-elimination). Follows Saytzeff's Rule (more highly substituted alkene is the major product).
  • Dehydration (from Alcohols): Heating with conc. H2SO4. Order of ease: 3° > 2° > 1°.

Chemical Properties:

Undergo Electrophilic Addition reactions.

🚨 Exam Facts: Addition Rules
  • Markovnikov's Rule: During addition of HX to an unsymmetrical alkene, the negative part of the addendum (X-) goes to that carbon which has fewer hydrogen atoms.
  • Anti-Markovnikov's Rule (Kharasch / Peroxide Effect): Addition of HBr (ONLY HBr) in presence of peroxide goes opposite to Markovnikov's rule. Proceeds via Free Radical mechanism.

Ozonolysis: Reaction with O3 followed by Zn/H2O breaks the double bond entirely, forming aldehydes or ketones. (Great for locating the position of double bond!).

🔥 3. Alkynes

Unsaturated hydrocarbons with a triple bond. General formula: CnH2n-2. The sp hybridized carbon is highly electronegative.

Key Reactions:

  • Acidic Character: Terminal alkynes (e.g., ethyne) have acidic hydrogen. React with Na metal to form sodium acetylide and H2 gas. Do not react with NaOH.
  • Addition of Water (Hydration): Alkynes + H2O (in dil. H2SO4 and HgSO4 at 333K) → Enol → Ketone (or Aldehyde for ethyne).
  • Cyclic Polymerization: Ethyne passed through red hot iron tube at 873K polymerizes to form Benzene.

⌬ 4. Aromatic Hydrocarbons (Benzene)

Hückel's Rule of Aromaticity

For a compound to be aromatic, it must be:

  1. Cyclic and Planar.
  2. Completely conjugated (delocalized π electrons in the ring).
  3. Must contain (4n + 2) π electrons, where n = 0, 1, 2... (e.g., 2, 6, 10 π electrons).

Electrophilic Substitution Reactions

Benzene is electron-rich due to the π-electron cloud, hence it attracts electrophiles (E+).

  • Nitration: Conc. HNO3 + Conc. H2SO4 (Nitrating mixture). Electrophile: NO2+.
  • Halogenation: Cl2 + Anhydrous AlCl3. Electrophile: Cl+.
  • Friedel-Crafts Alkylation: R-Cl + Anhydrous AlCl3. Electrophile: R+.
  • Friedel-Crafts Acylation: R-COCl + Anhydrous AlCl3. Electrophile: RCO+ (Acylium ion).
💡 Activating vs Deactivating Groups

Activating (Ortho/Para directing): Push electrons into ring (+R effect). E.g., -OH, -NH2, -OR, -CH3.
Deactivating (Meta directing): Pull electrons from ring (-R effect). E.g., -NO2, -CHO, -COOH, -CN.
Exception: Halogens (-Cl, -Br) are deactivating (due to strong -I) but are Ortho/Para directing (due to +R).
