Classification of Elements and Periodicity

Class 11 Chemistry | Chapter 3
🎯 Target: NEET UG | Fully NCERT Based

📜 1. Genesis of Periodic Classification

  • Dobereiner's Triads: Group of 3 elements where atomic weight of middle element is roughly the average of the other two. (e.g., Li, Na, K).
  • Newlands' Law of Octaves: Every eighth element has properties similar to the first (like musical notes). Failed after Calcium.
  • Mendeleev's Periodic Law: The properties of the elements are a periodic function of their atomic weights. He left gaps for undiscovered elements (Eka-Aluminium → Gallium, Eka-Silicon → Germanium).
📘 Modern Periodic Law (Moseley)

The physical and chemical properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.

📊 2. Modern Periodic Table

Consists of 7 horizontal rows (Periods) and 18 vertical columns (Groups).

Electronic Configurations in Blocks

  • s-block: Group 1 (Alkali metals) & Group 2 (Alkaline earth metals). General config: ns1-2. Highly reactive metals.
  • p-block: Group 13 to 18. General config: ns2 np1-6. Together with s-block, they are called Representative elements.
  • d-block: Group 3 to 12. General config: (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2. Transition elements. Form colored ions, variable valency, catalysts.
  • f-block: Lanthanoids (4f) and Actinoids (5f). General config: (n-2)f1-14 (n-1)d0-1 ns2. Inner transition elements. Actinoids are radioactive.

📏 3. Periodic Trends in Properties

1. Atomic Radius

Decreases across a period: Due to increasing effective nuclear charge (Zeff) pulling electrons closer.
Increases down a group: Due to addition of new shells and shielding effect.

Note: Isoelectronic species (same electrons). Larger positive charge → smaller radius. Larger negative charge → larger radius. (e.g., N3- > O2- > F- > Na+ > Mg2+ > Al3+).

2. Ionization Enthalpy (ΔiH)

Energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom.

Increases across a period. Decreases down a group.

🚨 Exam Facts: Exceptions in ΔiH

Be > B (because 2s is completely filled in Be, stable).
N > O (because 2p is exactly half-filled in N, highly stable).

3. Electron Gain Enthalpy (ΔegH)

Enthalpy change when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom. It is usually negative (exothermic).

Halogens have maximum negative ΔegH. Noble gases have positive ΔegH.

🤯 Amazing Info: Chlorine vs Fluorine

Chlorine has the highest negative electron gain enthalpy in the periodic table, not Fluorine! Due to the very small size of F, added electrons experience strong interelectronic repulsion.

4. Electronegativity (EN)

Tendency of an atom in a molecule to attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself. (Pauling scale: F=4.0, O=3.5, N=3.0, C=2.5).
Increases across a period. Decreases down a group.

🧪 4. Chemical Reactivity and Diagonal Relationship

Diagonal Relationship: The first element of certain groups shows similarity with the second element of the following group. (e.g., Li-Mg, Be-Al, B-Si). It is due to their similar ionic sizes and similar charge/radius ratio (polarizing power).

Acidic and Basic Nature of Oxides

  • Metals (Left side): Form Basic oxides (e.g., Na2O).
  • Non-metals (Right side): Form Acidic oxides (e.g., Cl2O7, SO3).
  • Middle/Diagonal elements: Form Amphoteric oxides (react with both acid and base). e.g., Al2O3, ZnO, PbO, BeO.
  • Neutral Oxides: N2O, NO, CO.
