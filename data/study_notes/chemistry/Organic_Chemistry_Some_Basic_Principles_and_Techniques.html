🧱 1. Tetravalence of Carbon

Carbon has 4 valence electrons. It forms four strong covalent bonds (catenation property) leading to millions of organic compounds.

  • sp3 Hybridization: All single bonds (e.g., Alkanes). Tetrahedral geometry. Bond angle 109.5°. (25% s-character).
  • sp2 Hybridization: One double bond (e.g., Alkenes). Trigonal planar. Bond angle 120°. (33.3% s-character).
  • sp Hybridization: One triple bond or two double bonds (e.g., Alkynes). Linear. Bond angle 180°. (50% s-character).

Note: Higher s-character means electrons are held closer to the nucleus, making that carbon more electronegative. (Electronegativity: sp > sp2 > sp3).

🔀 2. Isomerism

Compounds having same molecular formula but different physical or chemical properties.

Structural Isomerism

  • Chain Isomerism: Different carbon skeleton (e.g., n-pentane, isopentane, neopentane).
  • Position Isomerism: Different position of functional group or multiple bond (e.g., 1-butene and 2-butene).
  • Functional Isomerism: Different functional groups (e.g., Alcohol & Ether, Aldehyde & Ketone, Acid & Ester).
  • Metamerism: Different alkyl chains on either side of a polyvalent functional group (e.g., Ethers, Ketones, Amines).

Stereoisomerism (Brief)

Includes Geometrical (Cis-Trans) and Optical isomerism, which differ in spatial arrangement of atoms.

💥 3. Cleavage of Covalent Bond

  • Homolytic Cleavage: Bond breaks symmetrically. Each atom takes one electron. Forms Free Radicals. Requires heat or light (UV).
    Stability order of radicals: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl.
  • Heterolytic Cleavage: Bond breaks asymmetrically. More electronegative atom takes both electrons. Forms Carbocations (+) and Carbanions (-).
    Stability of Carbocations: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl (due to +I effect and hyperconjugation).
    Stability of Carbanions: methyl > 1° > 2° > 3°.

🧲 4. Electronic Effects in Organic Molecules

1. Inductive Effect (I Effect)

Permanent displacement of sigma (σ) electrons along a saturated carbon chain due to electronegativity difference. Decreases rapidly with distance.

  • -I Effect (Electron Withdrawing): -NO2, -CN, -COOH, -F, -Cl, -Br, -I. Increases acidity.
  • +I Effect (Electron Donating): Alkyl groups (-CH3, -C2H5). Decreases acidity, increases basicity.

2. Resonance Effect (R or M Effect)

Delocalization of pi (π) electrons or lone pairs in a conjugated system.

  • +R Effect: Groups that donate electrons to the conjugated system (usually have lone pairs). E.g., -OH, -OR, -NH2, -Cl. They are ortho/para directing.
  • -R Effect: Groups that withdraw electrons from the conjugated system (usually have double/triple bonds with electronegative atoms). E.g., -NO2, -CHO, -COOH, -CN. They are meta directing.

3. Hyperconjugation (No-Bond Resonance)

Delocalization of σ electrons of C-H bond of an alkyl group directly attached to an unsaturated system or carbocation/free radical. More alpha-hydrogens (α-H) = more hyperconjugation structures = more stability.

🚨 Exam Facts: Electromeric Effect (E Effect)

Unlike I, R, and Hyperconjugation which are permanent, the Electromeric effect is a temporary effect. It involves complete transfer of pi electrons to one of the atoms in the presence of an attacking reagent.

🧪 5. Purification and Characterization

Qualitative Analysis

  • Lassaigne's Test: Organic compound fused with Na metal to convert covalent elements into ionic salts (NaCN, Na2S, NaX).
  • Nitrogen: Detected by Prussian blue color (Formation of Ferric ferrocyanide).
  • Sulfur: Violet color with Sodium nitroprusside, or black ppt with Lead acetate.
  • Halogens: White/Pale yellow ppt with AgNO3 (Beilstein test / Lassaigne's extract).

Quantitative Analysis

Dumas Method & Kjeldahl's Method: For estimating Nitrogen percentage.

Carius Method: For Halogens and Sulfur.
