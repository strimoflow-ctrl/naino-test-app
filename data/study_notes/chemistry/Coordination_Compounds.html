Coordination Compounds

Class 12 Chemistry | Chapter 5

🔗 1. Terminology and Werner's Theory

Coordination Complex: A central metal atom or ion surrounded by a set of ligands (anions or neutral molecules) attached via coordinate covalent bonds.

  • Werner's Theory: Metals show two types of valency.
    • Primary Valency: Ionizable, corresponds to oxidation state. Satisfied by negative ions.
    • Secondary Valency: Non-ionizable, corresponds to coordination number. Satisfied by ligands. Directed in space giving definite geometry.
  • Ligands: Lewis bases that donate electron pairs to the metal. Can be unidentate (NH3, Cl-), didentate (en, C2O42-), or hexadentate (EDTA4-).
  • Chelation: Formation of ring structure by multidentate ligands. Greatly increases the stability of the complex (Chelate effect).

🔀 2. Isomerism in Complexes

Structural Isomerism

  • Ionization: Interchange of ligands between coordination sphere and ionization sphere. e.g., [Co(NH3)5Br]SO4 and [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br.
  • Hydrate (Solvate): Water molecules as ligands vs water of hydration. e.g., [Cr(H2O)6]Cl3 (Violet) and [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2•H2O (Green).
  • Linkage: Arises in ambidentate ligands (NO2, SCN). e.g., -NO2 (Nitro) vs -ONO (Nitrito).
  • Coordination: Interchange of ligands between cationic and anionic complex entities. e.g., [Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6].

Stereoisomerism

  • Geometrical: Cis (similar ligands adjacent) and Trans (similar ligands opposite). Common in Square Planar (MA2B2) and Octahedral (MA4B2).
  • Optical: Chiral complexes that lack a plane of symmetry. Trans isomers are usually optically inactive due to a plane of symmetry. Cis isomers can be optically active.

⚡ 3. Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

Uses hybridization to explain geometry and magnetic properties.

🚨 Exam Facts: Strong vs Weak Field Ligands (VBT)
  • Strong Ligands (e.g., CO, CN-, NH3, en): Force pairing of electrons against Hund's rule. Usually form Inner Orbital Complexes (d2sp3 for CN=6, dsp2 for CN=4). Usually diamagnetic or low spin.
  • Weak Ligands (e.g., Halogens, H2O, OH-): Cannot force pairing. Form Outer Orbital Complexes (sp3d2 for CN=6, sp3 for CN=4). Usually paramagnetic or high spin.

🌈 4. Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

Considers metal-ligand bond as purely ionic. The ligands are treated as point charges which cause splitting of the degenerate d-orbitals of the metal.

Octahedral Splitting (Δo)

The 5 d-orbitals split into two sets:

  • t2g (lower energy): dxy, dyz, dzx. (Between the axes).
  • eg (higher energy): dx2-y2, dz2. (Along the axes).
💡 Spectrochemical Series

Arrangement of ligands in increasing order of field strength (Δ).

I- < Br- < SCN- < Cl- < F- < OH- < C2O42- < H2O < NH3 < en < NO2- < CN- < CO

If Δo > P (pairing energy), pairing occurs → Low spin complex.
If Δo < P (pairing energy), electrons go to eg → High spin complex.

Color in Coordination Compounds

Due to d-d transition of electrons from lower energy t2g to higher energy eg level by absorbing a specific wavelength of visible light. The transmitted light is the complementary color.
