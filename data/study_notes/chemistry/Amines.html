🛠️ 1. Preparation of Amines

  • Reduction of Nitro Compounds: R-NO2 can be reduced to R-NH2 using Sn/HCl, Fe/HCl, or H2/Pd. (Fe/HCl is preferred as the FeCl2 formed gets hydrolyzed to release HCl during the reaction).
  • Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation: An amide reacts with Br2 and aqueous/ethanolic NaOH to form a 1° amine. The amine has one carbon less than the starting amide. (R-CONH2 → R-NH2).
  • Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis: Used specifically for the preparation of pure Primary (1°) Aliphatic Amines. (Note: Aromatic 1° amines like Aniline cannot be prepared by this method because aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution easily).

⚖️ 2. Basic Character of Amines

Amines act as Lewis bases due to the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom.

Basicity in Gas Phase vs Aqueous Phase

  • Gas Phase: Exclusively depends on +I effect of alkyl groups. Order: 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3.
  • Aqueous Phase: Depends on +I effect, Steric hindrance, and Solvation (hydration) effect.
    • For Methyl (-CH3) substituted amines: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3 (i.e., 213).
    • For Ethyl (-C2H5) substituted amines: 2° > 3° > 1° > NH3 (i.e., 231).
🚨 Exam Facts: Aniline Basicity

Aniline is weaker base than ammonia. The lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the benzene ring by resonance, making it less available for protonation.

🧪 3. Important Chemical Reactions

  • Carbylamine Reaction (Isocyanide Test): Only Primary (1°) Amines (both aliphatic and aromatic) react with Chloroform (CHCl3) and alcoholic KOH to form foul-smelling isocyanides/carbylamines. Used as a test for 1° amines.
  • Hinsberg Test: Used to distinguish 1°, 2°, and 3° amines. Reagent: Benzenesulphonyl chloride (C6H5SO2Cl).
    • 1° Amine: Forms a product soluble in alkali.
    • 2° Amine: Forms a product insoluble in alkali.
    • 3° Amine: Does not react.
  • Reaction with Nitrous Acid (HNO2 or NaNO2/HCl):
    • Aliphatic 1° amines: Form unstable diazonium salts which immediately decompose to yield alcohols and N2 gas.
    • Aromatic 1° amines (Aniline): Form stable (at 0-5°C) Benzene Diazonium Chloride.

🔗 4. Diazonium Salts (Ar-N2+Cl-)

Excellent intermediate for preparing various aromatic compounds.

  • Sandmeyer's Reaction: Replaces diazo group with -Cl, -Br, or -CN using Cu(I) salts (Cu2Cl2, Cu2Br2, CuCN).
  • Gattermann Reaction: Uses copper powder in presence of corresponding acid (Cu/HCl, Cu/HBr).
  • Coupling Reaction: Diazonium salt reacts with phenol or aniline to form highly colored Azo compounds (Dyes). With Phenol (in basic medium), it forms orange dye. With Aniline (in acidic medium), it forms yellow dye.
