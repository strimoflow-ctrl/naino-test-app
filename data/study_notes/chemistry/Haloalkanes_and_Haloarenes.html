Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

Class 12 Chemistry | Chapter 6
🎯 Target: NEET UG | Fully NCERT Based

🧪 1. Methods of Preparation

Haloalkanes (R-X)

  • From Alcohols: Best method is using SOCl2 (Thionyl chloride) because byproducts (SO2, HCl) are escapable gases. R-OH + SOCl2 → R-Cl + SO2 + HCl.
  • From Hydrocarbons: Free radical halogenation of alkanes. Electrophilic addition of HX to alkenes (Markovnikov's rule).
  • Halogen Exchange:
    • Finkelstein Reaction: R-X + NaI → R-I + NaX (in dry acetone). Used for making Alkyl Iodides.
    • Swarts Reaction: Used for making Alkyl Fluorides using AgF, Hg2F2, CoF2.

Haloarenes (Ar-X)

  • Electrophilic Substitution: Halogenation of benzene in dark with Lewis acid (FeCl3).
  • Sandmeyer's Reaction: From diazonium salts using Cu2Cl2/HCl or Cu2Br2/HBr. (If only Cu powder is used, it is called Gattermann Reaction).

🔀 2. Nucleophilic Substitution Reactions (SN1 vs SN2)

The core reaction of haloalkanes where a nucleophile replaces the halogen atom.

💡 Comparison of Mechanisms
Feature SN1 SN2
Steps Two Steps (Carbocation intermediate) One Step (Transition state)
Kinetics First order (Rate depends only on RX) Second order (Rate depends on RX and Nu)
Reactivity Order 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3X (Stability of C+) CH3X > 1° > 2° > 3° (Steric hindrance)
Stereochemistry Racemization (Inversion + Retention) Complete Inversion (Walden Inversion)
Solvent Polar Protic (H2O, Alcohols) Polar Aprotic (Acetone, DMF)

⚠️ 3. Chemical Reactions of Haloalkanes

  • Elimination Reactions (β-elimination): React with alcoholic KOH to give alkenes. Follows Saytzeff's Rule. (Note: Aqueous KOH gives Substitution i.e., Alcohols).
  • Reaction with Metals:
    • Grignard Reagent: R-X + Mg (dry ether) → R-Mg-X. (Highly reactive, must be kept dry as it reacts with water to form alkanes).
    • Wurtz Reaction: Discussed in Hydrocarbons (gives symmetrical alkanes).

🛡️ 4. Properties of Haloarenes

Haloarenes are extremely less reactive towards nucleophilic substitution reactions compared to haloalkanes. Reasons:

  1. Resonance Effect: The lone pair on halogen is in conjugation with benzene ring, imparting partial double bond character to the C-X bond, making it difficult to break.
  2. sp2 hybridized Carbon: More electronegative, holds the electron pair of C-X bond more tightly.
🚨 Exam Facts: Dow's Process

Chlorobenzene can be converted to phenol only under drastic conditions (623 K, 300 atm) with NaOH. However, presence of Electron Withdrawing Groups (-NO2) at ortho/para positions makes this substitution much easier.

Electrophilic Substitution

Halogens are deactivating but Ortho/Para directing due to resonance (+R effect overrides -I effect for orientation, but -I overrides +R for reactivity).
