⏱️ 1. Rate of Reaction

The change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.

For reaction: aA + bB → cC + dD

Rate = -(1/a) d[A]/dt = -(1/b) d[B]/dt = +(1/c) d[C]/dt = +(1/d) d[D]/dt

Rate Law: An experimentally determined expression relating rate to reactant concentrations. Rate = k [A]x [B]y.

  • Order of Reaction: Sum of powers of concentration terms in rate law (x + y). It can be 0, fraction, or integer. Experimentally determined.
  • Molecularity: Number of reacting species colliding simultaneously in an elementary step. It must be a whole number (1, 2, or 3). Cannot be zero or fractional. Theoretical concept.

📉 2. Integrated Rate Equations

Zero Order Reaction

Rate is independent of concentration of reactants. Rate = k [A]° = k.

Equation: [A] = [A]0 - kt

Half-life (t1/2): t1/2 = [A]0 / 2k. (Half life is directly proportional to initial concentration).

Examples: Decomposition of gases on metal surfaces at high pressure (e.g., NH3 on Pt).

First Order Reaction

Rate is directly proportional to the first power of reactant concentration. Rate = k [A]1.

Equation: k = (2.303 / t) log ([A]0 / [A])

Half-life (t1/2): t1/2 = 0.693 / k. (Half life is INDEPENDENT of initial concentration).

Examples: All natural and artificial radioactive decays.

🚨 Exam Facts: First Order Tricks

Time required for 99.9% completion = 10 × t1/2

Time required for 99% completion = 6.66 × t1/2

Amount left after 'n' half-lives = Initial Amount / 2n

🧬 3. Pseudo First Order Reactions

Reactions which are not truly of first order but behave as first order under certain conditions (usually when one reactant is in large excess).

Example: Acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of ethyl acetate or inversion of cane sugar.

CH3COOC2H5 + H2O (excess) → CH3COOH + C2H5OH.

Rate = k' [Ester][H2O]. Since [H2O] is constant, Rate = k [Ester].

🔥 4. Temperature Dependence (Arrhenius Equation)

For most chemical reactions, a 10°C rise in temperature nearly doubles the rate of reaction (Temperature Coefficient ≈ 2).

💡 Arrhenius Equation

k = A e-Ea / RT

Where:
A = Arrhenius Factor or Frequency Factor.
Ea = Activation Energy (Minimum extra amount of energy absorbed by reactant molecules to form activated complex).
e-Ea/RT = Fraction of molecules having energy equal to or greater than Ea.

Logarithmic form for two temperatures:
log (k2 / k1) = (Ea / 2.303R) [ (T2 - T1) / (T1 T2) ]

Effect of Catalyst

A catalyst increases the rate of reaction by providing an alternative pathway with a lower Activation Energy (Ea). It does not alter ΔH, ΔG, or Equilibrium constant (K) of the reaction.
