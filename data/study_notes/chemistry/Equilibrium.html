⚖️ 1. Chemical Equilibrium

A state in a reversible reaction where the rate of forward reaction equals the rate of backward reaction. The concentration of reactants and products becomes constant.

💡 Equilibrium Constant (Kc and Kp)

For a reaction: aA + bB ↔ cC + dD

Kc = [C]c [D]d / ([A]a [B]b)

Kp = (PC)c (PD)d / ((PA)a (PB)b)

Relation: Kp = Kc (RT)Δng

Where Δng = gaseous products - gaseous reactants.

Note: Pure solids and pure liquids are taken as unity (1) in the equilibrium constant expression.

🔄 2. Le Chatelier's Principle

If a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change of concentration, pressure, or temperature, the system shifts in a direction that tends to undo the effect of the change.

  • Concentration: Increasing reactants shifts equilibrium Forward. Removing products shifts Forward.
  • Pressure: Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium towards the side with fewer gaseous moles.
  • Temperature: Increasing temperature favors the Endothermic direction. Decreasing temperature favors the Exothermic direction.
  • Catalyst: Has NO EFFECT on the equilibrium state (K remains same), it only helps attain equilibrium faster.
  • Inert Gas Addition: At constant Volume: No effect. At constant Pressure: Shifts equilibrium towards more gaseous moles.

🧪 3. Ionic Equilibrium (Acids and Bases)

  • Arrhenius Concept: Acid gives H+ in water. Base gives OH- in water.
  • Brønsted-Lowry Concept: Acid is a proton (H+) donor. Base is a proton acceptor. (Conjugate acid-base pairs differ by one H+).
  • Lewis Concept: Acid is an electron pair acceptor (e.g., BF3, AlCl3). Base is an electron pair donor (e.g., NH3, H2O).

Ionic Product of Water (Kw)

Kw = [H+][OH-] = 10-14 at 298 K.

pH = -log[H+]. At 298K, pH + pOH = 14.

🚨 Exam Facts: Temperature effect on pure water

When temperature increases, dissociation of water increases. Kw increases > 10-14. Both [H+] and [OH-] increase. Therefore, pH of pure water becomes LESS THAN 7 at higher temperatures (but it remains neutral because [H+] = [OH-]).

🛡️ 4. Buffer Solutions

Solutions that resist a change in pH upon the addition of small amounts of acid or base.

  • Acidic Buffer: Weak acid + its salt with strong base. (e.g., CH3COOH + CH3COONa).
    pH = pKa + log([Salt]/[Acid]) (Henderson-Hasselbalch eq)
  • Basic Buffer: Weak base + its salt with strong acid. (e.g., NH4OH + NH4Cl).
    pOH = pKb + log([Salt]/[Base])

Maximum buffer capacity is achieved when [Salt] = [Acid], meaning pH = pKa.

🌊 5. Solubility Product (Ksp)

For a sparingly soluble salt AxBy(s) ↔ x Ay+(aq) + y Bx-(aq)

Ksp = [Ay+]x [Bx-]y

Let solubility be 's' mol/L. Then Ksp = (xs)x (ys)y = xx yy sx+y

📘 Precipitation Condition

Let Ionic Product (IP) or Q be the product of ionic concentrations at any instant.

  • If IP < Ksp: Solution is unsaturated (No precipitation).
  • If IP = Ksp: Solution is saturated (Equilibrium).
  • If IP > Ksp: Solution is supersaturated (Precipitation occurs).
