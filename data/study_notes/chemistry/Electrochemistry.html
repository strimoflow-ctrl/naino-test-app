🔋 1. Electrochemical Cells

A device that converts chemical energy of a spontaneous redox reaction into electrical energy (Galvanic Cell). E.g., Daniell Cell.

Cell Potential (EMF): The potential difference between two electrodes of a galvanic cell.

E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode (Standard reduction potentials).

💡 Trick: L-O-A-N

Left = Oxidation = Anode = Negative pole.

Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE): Used as a reference electrode. Its standard reduction potential is arbitrarily taken as 0.00 V at all temperatures.

⚡ 2. Nernst Equation

Used to calculate cell potential under non-standard conditions (concentration other than 1M).

💡 Nernst Equation (at 298 K)

Ecell = E°cell - (0.0591 / n) log Q

Where n = number of electrons transferred, Q = reaction quotient = [Products] / [Reactants].

At equilibrium, Ecell = 0, and Q = Kc. So, E°cell = (0.0591 / n) log Kc.

Gibbs Free Energy: ΔG° = -n F E°cell (where F = Faraday constant = 96487 C mol-1). For a spontaneous reaction, ΔG° must be negative, so E°cell must be positive.

🌊 3. Conductance of Electrolytic Solutions

  • Conductance (G): Reciprocal of resistance. G = 1/R. Unit: Siemens (S) or ohm-1.
  • Conductivity (κ - kappa): Reciprocal of resistivity. κ = 1/ρ = G × (l/A) = G × Cell constant (G*).
  • Molar Conductivity (Λm): Conducting power of all ions produced by dissolving 1 mole of electrolyte.
    Λm = (κ × 1000) / Molarity
🚨 Exam Facts: Effect of Dilution

As dilution increases (concentration decreases):

  • Conductivity (κ) DECREASES because the number of ions per unit volume decreases.
  • Molar Conductivity (Λm) INCREASES because total volume containing 1 mole of electrolyte increases. For weak electrolytes, it increases steeply at infinite dilution due to increased degree of dissociation.

📏 4. Kohlrausch's Law

Law of independent migration of ions. At infinite dilution, each ion makes a definite contribution towards molar conductivity of the electrolyte irrespective of the nature of the other ion.

Λ°m (AB) = λ°m (A+) + λ°m (B-)

Application: Used to determine the limiting molar conductivity of weak electrolytes (like CH3COOH) using strong electrolytes (like CH3COONa, HCl, NaCl).

⚗️ 5. Electrolytic Cells and Faraday's Laws

Electrical energy is used to carry out non-spontaneous chemical reactions.

  • Faraday's First Law: The mass of substance deposited or liberated at any electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity (charge, Q) passed. m = Z × Q = Z × I × t (where Z is electrochemical equivalent).
  • Faraday's Second Law: When same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytes connected in series, the masses of substances produced are proportional to their equivalent weights. m1 / m2 = E1 / E2.

1 Faraday (1F) = Charge on 1 mole of electrons ≈ 96500 C. It deposits 1 equivalent weight of a substance.
