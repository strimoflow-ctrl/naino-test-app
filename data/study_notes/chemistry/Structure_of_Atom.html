🔬 1. Subatomic Particles

  • Electron (e⁻): Discovered by J.J. Thomson (Cathode Ray Experiment). Charge = -1.6 × 10-19 C. Mass = 9.1 × 10-31 kg.
  • Proton (p⁺): Discovered by Goldstein (Canal Rays). Charge = +1.6 × 10-19 C. Mass = 1.672 × 10-27 kg.
  • Neutron (n): Discovered by Chadwick. Neutral charge. Mass = 1.675 × 10-27 kg (slightly heavier than proton).
🤯 Amazing Info: Millikan's Oil Drop

R.A. Millikan performed the oil drop experiment to determine the exact charge of an electron. He found that the charge was always an integral multiple of a basic minimum value, proving the quantization of charge.

🌀 2. Bohr's Model for Hydrogen Atom

Bohr overcame Rutherford's limitations by applying Planck's quantum theory. He postulated that electrons revolve in stationary orbits with fixed energy.

💡 Important Formulas (For Hydrogen and H-like species)

Radius: rn = 52.9 × (n2 / Z) pm

Energy: En = -2.18 × 10-18 × (Z2 / n2) J = -13.6 × (Z2 / n2) eV

Velocity: vn = 2.18 × 106 × (Z / n) m/s

Limitations of Bohr's Model: Failed to explain the spectra of multi-electron atoms. Failed to explain the fine splitting of spectral lines in magnetic field (Zeeman effect) and electric field (Stark effect). Also violates Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle.

🌊 3. Dual Behavior and Uncertainty Principle

de Broglie Equation

All material particles in motion possess wave characteristics.

λ = h / p = h / (mv)

Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle

It is impossible to determine simultaneously, the exact position and exact momentum (or velocity) of an electron.

Δx × Δp ≥ h / 4π

Or: Δx × (m Δv) ≥ h / 4π

🎯 4. Quantum Mechanical Model

Developed by Erwin Schrödinger. The probability of finding an electron is high in regions called Orbitals.

Quantum Numbers

A set of four numbers used to completely describe the position and energy of the electron.

  1. Principal (n): Shell. Size and Energy. n = 1, 2, 3... (K, L, M...). Number of orbitals in a shell = n2. Max electrons = 2n2.
  2. Azimuthal/Angular Momentum (l): Subshell. Shape of orbital. l = 0 to (n-1). (l=0 is s, l=1 is p, l=2 is d, l=3 is f). Orbital angular momentum = √[l(l+1)] (h/2π).
  3. Magnetic (ml): Orientation of orbital. ml = -l to +l (including zero). Total values = (2l + 1).
  4. Spin (ms): Spin of electron. ms = +1/2 or -1/2.

📝 5. Rules for Filling Electrons

  • Aufbau Principle: In the ground state, orbitals are filled in order of their increasing energies (Lowest energy first). Follows the (n+l) rule.
  • Pauli Exclusion Principle: No two electrons in an atom can have the same set of all four quantum numbers. (An orbital can hold max 2 electrons with opposite spins).
  • Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity: Pairing of electrons in the orbitals belonging to the same subshell (p, d, f) does not take place until each orbital is singly occupied.
🚨 Exam Facts: Exceptional Configurations

Half-filled and fully-filled subshells have extra stability due to symmetry and exchange energy.

Cr (Z=24): [Ar] 4s1 3d5 (NOT 4s2 3d4)

Cu (Z=29): [Ar] 4s1 3d10 (NOT 4s2 3d9)
