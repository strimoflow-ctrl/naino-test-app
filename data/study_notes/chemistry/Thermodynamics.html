Thermodynamics

🌡️ 1. Basic Concepts

Thermodynamics deals with energy changes in physical and chemical processes.

  • System: The part of the universe chosen for thermodynamic observation.
  • Surroundings: Everything else in the universe except the system.
  • Boundary: Real or imaginary surface separating system from surroundings.

Types of Systems

  • Open: Exchanges both matter and energy with surroundings (e.g., boiling water in an open cup).
  • Closed: Exchanges energy but not matter (e.g., boiling water in a sealed flask).
  • Isolated: Exchanges neither energy nor matter (e.g., thermos flask).

State vs Path Functions

State Functions: Depend only on initial and final states, not on the path taken. (e.g., U, H, S, G, P, V, T).

Path Functions: Depend on the path taken. (e.g., Work (w) and Heat (q)).

⚡ 2. First Law of Thermodynamics

The energy of an isolated system is constant. (Energy can neither be created nor destroyed).

💡 Mathematical Form (IUPAC Convention)

ΔU = q + w

  • ΔU: Change in internal energy.
  • q: Heat (+q means heat absorbed BY system, -q means heat released BY system).
  • w: Work (+w means work done ON system, -w means work done BY system).

Work done in isothermal reversible expansion:
w = -2.303 nRT log(V2/V1) = -2.303 nRT log(P1/P2)

🔥 3. Enthalpy (H) and Heat Capacity

Enthalpy (H) is the total heat content of a system at constant pressure.

H = U + PV

At constant pressure, ΔH = qp (Heat exchanged at constant P).
At constant volume, ΔU = qv (Heat exchanged at constant V).

Relation: ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT

Where Δng = (moles of gaseous products) - (moles of gaseous reactants).

Heat Capacity (C)

Amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a substance by 1°C.

  • Specific Heat Capacity (c): For 1 gram of substance. (q = m × c × ΔT).
  • Molar Heat Capacity (Cm): For 1 mole of substance. (CP - CV = R for ideal gas).

🔁 4. Second Law and Entropy (S)

The entropy of the universe is continuously increasing in any spontaneous process.

Entropy (S): A measure of randomness or disorder in the system. Solid < Liquid < Gas.

ΔS = qrev / T

🚨 Exam Facts: Spontaneity

For a process to be spontaneous, the total entropy change (system + surroundings) must be positive.

ΔStotal = ΔSsystem + ΔSsurr > 0

⚖️ 5. Gibbs Free Energy (G)

Gibbs Free Energy is the energy available to do useful work. It combines enthalpy and entropy into a single criterion for spontaneity.

💡 Gibbs Equation

ΔG = ΔH - TΔS

Criteria for Spontaneity at constant T and P:

  • If ΔG < 0 (Negative): Process is Spontaneous.
  • If ΔG > 0 (Positive): Process is Non-spontaneous.
  • If ΔG = 0: System is in Equilibrium.

Relation with Equilibrium Constant (K)

ΔG° = -2.303 RT log(K)

If K > 1, ΔG° is negative (products favored). If K < 1, ΔG° is positive (reactants favored).
