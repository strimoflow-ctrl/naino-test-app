💧 1. Henry's Law

States that at a constant temperature, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas present above the surface of liquid or solution.

p = KH × x

Where p is partial pressure, x is mole fraction of gas in solution, and KH is Henry's law constant.

🚨 Exam Facts: Henry's Constant (KH)

Higher the value of KH at a given pressure, lower is the solubility of the gas in the liquid. Also, KH increases with an increase in temperature, which means solubility of gases in liquids decreases with increase in temperature. (This is why aquatic species are more comfortable in cold waters!).

🧪 2. Raoult's Law & Ideal Solutions

For a solution of volatile liquids, the partial vapour pressure of each component is directly proportional to its mole fraction in solution.

pA = pA° × xA

Ideal Solutions

Obey Raoult's law over the entire range of concentration. Formed when A-B interactions are nearly same as A-A and B-B interactions. (e.g., n-hexane + n-heptane, benzene + toluene).

ΔHmix = 0 ; ΔVmix = 0.

Non-Ideal Solutions

  • Positive Deviation: A-B interactions are weaker than A-A/B-B. Vapour pressure is higher than expected. ΔHmix > 0, ΔVmix > 0. Form Minimum boiling azeotropes. (e.g., Ethanol + Acetone).
  • Negative Deviation: A-B interactions are stronger than A-A/B-B. Vapour pressure is lower than expected. ΔHmix < 0, ΔVmix < 0. Form Maximum boiling azeotropes. (e.g., Nitric acid + Water).

⚖️ 3. Colligative Properties

Properties of dilute solutions containing non-volatile solute which depend ONLY on the number of solute particles and not on their nature.

  1. Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure: (p1° - p1) / p1° = x2 (Mole fraction of solute).
  2. Elevation of Boiling Point (ΔTb): The boiling point of a solution is always higher than that of the pure solvent.
    ΔTb = Kb × m (where Kb is Molal elevation constant, m is molality).
  3. Depression of Freezing Point (ΔTf): The freezing point of a solution is always lower than that of the pure solvent.
    ΔTf = Kf × m (where Kf is Molal depression constant / Cryoscopic constant).
  4. Osmotic Pressure (π): The excess pressure applied to the solution side to prevent osmosis (passage of solvent through a semi-permeable membrane).
    π = C R T (where C is molarity). Used to determine molar masses of polymers and proteins.

🧮 4. Abnormal Molar Mass & van't Hoff Factor (i)

When solute undergoes association or dissociation in solution, the calculated molar mass differs from the normal value.

van't Hoff factor (i) = Normal molar mass / Abnormal molar mass = Observed colligative property / Calculated colligative property.

  • For Dissociation (e.g., NaCl in water): i > 1. Number of particles increases.
  • For Association (e.g., Acetic acid in benzene forming dimers): i < 1. Number of particles decreases.
  • For non-electrolytes (e.g., Glucose, Urea): i = 1.
💡 Modified Equations

All colligative property formulas must be multiplied by 'i' when dealing with electrolytes.

ΔTb = i × Kb × m

π = i × C R T
