Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

Class 12 Chemistry | Chapter 7

🍷 1. Alcohols (R-OH)

Key Preparations:

  • From Alkenes: Acid catalyzed hydration gives Markovnikov addition. Hydroboration-Oxidation gives Anti-Markovnikov addition (forms 1° alcohols).
  • From Carbonyls: Reduction of Aldehydes (forms 1° alcohols) and Ketones (forms 2° alcohols) using LiAlH4 or NaBH4.
  • Using Grignard Reagent:
    • Formaldehyde (HCHO) + RMgX → 1° Alcohol
    • Any other Aldehyde + RMgX → 2° Alcohol
    • Ketone + RMgX → 3° Alcohol

Chemical Properties:

Oxidation:

  • 1° Alcohols → Aldehydes (using PCC) → Carboxylic Acids (using strong agents like KMnO4/H+).
  • 2° Alcohols → Ketones (using CrO3 or PCC).
  • 3° Alcohols do not easily oxidize. With heated Cu at 573K, they undergo dehydration to form alkenes.
🚨 Exam Facts: Lucas Test

Used to distinguish 1°, 2°, and 3° alcohols. Reagent: Conc. HCl + Anhydrous ZnCl2.

  • 3° Alcohol: Turbidity appears immediately.
  • 2° Alcohol: Turbidity appears within 5 minutes.
  • 1° Alcohol: No turbidity at room temperature.

🧪 2. Phenols (Ar-OH)

Also known as Carbolic acid. Phenol is more acidic than alcohols due to the resonance stabilization of the phenoxide ion.

Key Preparations:

  • From Cumene (Isopropylbenzene): Cumene is oxidized to cumene hydroperoxide, which is then cleaved with dilute acid to give Phenol + Acetone (Acetone is a valuable byproduct).

Chemical Properties (Name Reactions):

  • Kolbe's Reaction: Phenol reacts with NaOH and CO2 (followed by acidification) to form Salicylic acid (used to make Aspirin).
  • Reimer-Tiemann Reaction: Phenol + CHCl3 + aq. NaOH → Salicylaldehyde.
  • Electrophilic Substitution: -OH group is strongly activating and Ortho/Para directing. With bromine water, it gives white ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenol. With dil. HNO3, it gives a mixture of o- and p-nitrophenol.

☁️ 3. Ethers (R-O-R')

Key Preparations:

Williamson Synthesis: R-X + R'-O-Na+ → R-O-R' + NaX. (SN2 mechanism).

⚠️ Crucial Condition

For Williamson synthesis to work for unsymmetrical ethers, the Alkyl Halide (R-X) MUST be primary (1°). If 3° alkyl halide is used, elimination competes and alkene is the major product instead of ether.

Chemical Properties:

Cleavage by HI: Ethers are cleaved by strong acids like HI at high temperatures.

R-O-R' + HI → R-I + R'-OH (If one group is 3°, the 3° group gets the Iodide due to stable SN1 carbocation. If both are 1°/2°, the smaller group gets the Iodide via SN2).

For Anisole (Methoxybenzene) + HI → Phenol + CH3I (O-Ar bond does not break due to partial double bond character).
